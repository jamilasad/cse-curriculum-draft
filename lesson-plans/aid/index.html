Draft 1, 24 November 2026. For review by the course teachers and the curriculum committee.

These files turn the four AID course outlines into class-by-class and session-by-session plans. The source of truth is curriculum/courses/62-track-aid.md (the course packages) and curriculum/00-framework.md. The lesson plans expand the outlines. They do not change any course code, title, credit, contact hour, prerequisite, CLO or weekly topic. Where an outline cannot be followed as written, the course file reports it in its last section, "Issues for the committee".

How each course file is laid out

Every course file has the same nine sections, so that a teacher who knows one file can find things in the others.

  1. Course facts. Copied from the outline without change: codes, titles, credits, contact hours, semester, prerequisites, AI-use levels, CLO table, minimum competency.
  2. Class pattern. The assumption in A1 below, applied to the course.
  3. Theory lesson plan. An overview table, then one entry for each of the 28 classes in the format of template T1 (section 4).
  4. Lab lesson plan. An overview table, then one entry for each of the 14 sessions in the format of template T2.
  5. Assignments and project brief. A full brief for each marked assignment and for the lab project.
  6. Assessment strategy. Components, week-by-week calendar, examination blueprint, sample questions with answers or marking notes, project and viva rubrics, the CLO-to-assessment-to-PO table, and CLO attainment.
  7. Support for weaker students.
  8. Course teacher responsibilities and minimum qualification. For the theory teacher and the lab instructor.
  9. Issues for the committee.

2. AI-use rules as applied in this track

The levels come from framework section 6. The track overview ("Compute, tools and AI use") applies them. The course header rows are the authority for each course.

LevelWhat it means in this trackWhere it applies
0No AI help of any kind.All quizzes, written examinations, supervised practical tests and vivas.
1AI as tutor. AI may explain a concept or an error message. It may not write code, derivations or answers. Inline code completion is switched off.Lab exercises marked by hand in the outlines, and unmarked by-hand practice sets.
2AI as pair programmer, with disclosure. Usage is logged, output is tested, and the student defends every line.Take-home assignments in all four courses. Lab work in CSE-4784 (except the by-hand weeks 1–3 and week 11).
3AI is the material. The model or AI system is the thing being built and measured. Coding assistants may still be used, under the Level 2 conditions.Lab work in CSE-3674, CSE-4774 and CSE-4884, and week 11 of CSE-4784.

Unmarked homework. The header rows set levels for marked work. Each class entry also gives a level for its homework. Practice problems that rehearse a skill tested at Level 0 (a derivation, a hand computation, SQL on paper) are labelled "by hand, Level 1", following the track rule that by-hand exercises are Level 1. Reading and notebook homework is labelled Level 2.

Disclosure. Every repository has a file AI-USAGE.md (template T5). Every written assignment ends with a short AI-use statement: tool, what it was used for, what the student changed, and how the student checked it. "No AI used" is also a statement.

Level 0 settings.

  • Supervised practical tests run on lab PCs with the network off, or limited to the lab file server where cached model weights or offline documentation are needed.
  • Allowed material: the offline library documentation the instructor has placed on the file server, and any one-page sheet the course file names. No phones, no personal laptops, no notes unless the course file says so.
  • Two invigilators for a section of 30 (the lab instructor and one other teacher or trained assistant).

Checking.

  • In every regular lab session the instructor asks some students one question about their own work ("why this split?", "what does this line do?"). The answer is part of the weekly mark (R1).
  • Level 1 by-hand work is done in the lab session wherever possible, and is checked by a short explanation at the bench.
  • The viva checks everything a student has submitted.

Breaches. Undisclosed AI use in Level 2 or Level 3 work, or any AI use at Level 0 or Level 1, is handled under the university's academic-integrity procedure. These plans do not set their own penalties. The course teacher keeps the evidence (the submission, the log, notes of the conversation with the student).

Data and AI services. Personal data, partner data and employer data are never pasted into a public AI service. This follows the capstone rule: client data needs the client's written consent first.

3. Shared marking rubrics

The course files use these rubrics and add course-specific criteria where needed. The totals do not change.

R1. Weekly lab work and lab record (30 marks)

Each course has 11 regular lab sessions (14 sessions minus two supervised tests and the viva session). Each regular session is marked 0–3. The best 10 of the 11 count, so the total is 30. The dropped session covers one absence or one bad week.

PointAwarded when
1. DeliverableThe deliverable runs from a clean start (a fresh notebook kernel, a clean checkout) and meets the lab sheet's specification.
2. ChecksThe check the sheet requires is present and correct: for example a gradient check, a reconciliation line, a leakage table, passing tests, a verified hand computation.
3. Record and explanationThe lab record entry is written (template T6), the AI-use lines are filled in, and the student answers a spot question about their own work, or the decision log answers it.
  • Late work: accepted until the start of the next session, for at most 2 of the 3 points.
  • Absence with a documented reason: the session becomes the dropped session. A second documented absence is made up in open-lab hours.
  • CSE-4884 weeks 1–6: the record is the commit history and automated test output (outline). They are marked at the week 7 check-off.

R2. Supervised practical tests (2 × 15 = 30 marks)

  • Level 0, on lab PCs, network off, offline documentation only (section 2).
  • 100 minutes of work inside the 2-hour session. The first 10 minutes are for setup and reading. The last 10 minutes are for submitting.
  • The task is unseen but of a type practised in the labs. Each course file gives the task, the mark split and a marking scheme.
  • Marks go to method and validation, not only to the final number. A correct number from a leaking or untested method earns little.
  • Make-up test (proposal for the department to confirm): one make-up sitting in week 14 or the examination period for a documented absence. It uses a parallel task of the same difficulty.

R3. Lab project (25 marks): frame

Each course file sets its own criteria within this frame. The levels are the same in all four courses.

LevelShare of a criterion's marksMeaning
Not yet acceptable0–39%Missing, wrong, or not the student's own work.
Acceptable40–69%Does what the brief asks. Checks are present. Limits are stated.
Good70–89%Sound choices with reasons. Evidence for each claim.
Strong90–100%Goes beyond the brief with evidence. Fit to show an employer.

Pairs and teams. Members receive the same project mark, unless the commit history, the AI-usage log and the viva show clearly unequal contribution. The instructor may then apply a factor of 0.8, 0.6 or 0.4 to one member's project mark, as the capstone scheme does. This is a proposal for the department to confirm. The member may appeal through the normal route.

R4. Individual viva (15 marks)

Level 0. About 10 minutes for each student. In pair or team projects, the team demonstrates first (about 8 minutes), then each member is questioned alone. Two examiners where the section is larger than A6 allows.

CriterionMarks0LowAcceptableStrong
A. Explains own submitted work: opens the repository and walks through a part they wrote5Cannot explain it1–2: reads the code aloud; cannot say why3–4: explains what and why for their part5: explains clearly, including what they would change
B. Justifies decisions and results: the split, the metric, the threshold, a design choice, what a number means4No reason given1: one reason, weak2–3: sound reasons with evidence4: weighs alternatives and limits
C. Handles a small change: "what happens if…?" or a small live edit3Cannot start1: guesses2: predicts correctly3: predicts and shows it

Pass rule. Framework section 7: a student who cannot explain their own submitted work does not pass the lab, whatever the project mark. These plans treat a score of 0 or 1 on criterion A, confirmed after a second question on a different part of the student's work, as "cannot explain". The threshold is a proposal for the department to confirm. The examiner records the questions and a summary of the answers on the viva form (template T11).

R6. Take-home assignment: frame

Each assignment brief gives its own criteria. All briefs share these rules:

  • The brief states the task, data, deliverables, deadline week, AI-use level, disclosure rule, the CLOs assessed and the rubric.
  • Submission through the course's learning platform or repository, with the AI-use statement.
  • The teacher may ask any student to explain a submitted answer in a 5-minute conversation. A student who cannot explain it is handled under the academic-integrity procedure.

Headings and sentence frames:

5. Assessment, moderation and outcome-based records

5.1 Written examination rules (framework section 7)

Every paper (mid-term and final) has a blueprint table before it is set: question, marks, CLO, PO, Bloom level. The blueprint must show:

  • at least 50% of the paper's marks at Apply or Analyse (application and analysis);
  • at most 20% at Remember (recall);
  • the rest at Understand, Evaluate or Create.

Each question part maps to one CLO, so that CLO marks can be added up without splitting. Quizzes follow the same rules where they can. They are short, so a single quiz may be all application.

5.2 Moderation

Proposals for the department to confirm:

  • A second teacher of the track checks each paper and its marking scheme against the blueprint before printing.
  • The marking scheme is fixed before marking starts.
  • A second marker re-marks a sample of scripts across the range of marks (for example, the top, middle and bottom five). Where the two marks differ by more than a tenth of the paper, the scripts in that range are re-marked.
  • Practical tests: the same, using the saved test scripts.
  • Vivas: two examiners where possible. Otherwise the viva form (T11) is completed during the viva, and a sample is re-heard or reviewed.
  • Project marks: a second teacher marks a sample of projects from the repository.

5.3 CLO attainment

The calculation uses the item map in T12.

  • Student CLO score. For student s and CLO k: the marks s obtained on all items mapped to k, divided by the maximum marks of those items, times 100.
  • Student attains the CLO when the score is at least 50%. This threshold is a proposal for the department to confirm.
  • Course CLO attainment. The share of students who sat the assessments and attained the CLO.
  • Target. At least 60% of students attain each CLO. This is a proposal for the department to confirm.
  • PO contribution. For each PO, the course reports the attainment of the CLOs mapped to it, each weighted by the marks behind that CLO.
  • Theory and lab. They are recorded separately, because they are separate 100-mark components. A CLO assessed in both is reported from both.
  • Optional items. Best-three-of-four quizzes: the item map uses the quizzes actually counted for each student.

Worked illustration (made-up numbers). A student's CLO 4 items carry 30 marks in all, and the student obtained 18. The student CLO 4 score is 18 / 30 × 100 = 60%, so CLO 4 is attained. If 41 of 55 students attain CLO 4, course attainment is 41 / 55 = 74.5%, above the 60% target.

Recording and use. The course teacher completes T12 within two weeks of the final. If a CLO falls below target, the course report states the likely cause (teaching, assessment design, or preparation) and one change for the next offering. The department's own OBE procedures, if they differ, take precedence. They must be checked before first use.

5.4 Evidence to keep for accreditation (the course file)

The course teacher keeps, for each offering:

  • the outline and this lesson plan as used, with changes noted;
  • the published one-page tool list;
  • the question papers, blueprints, marking schemes and moderation notes;
  • three marked scripts from the top, middle and bottom of the range for each written paper and practical test;
  • the lab sheets, the practical test tasks and their saved submissions;
  • the project briefs, rubrics and a sample of marked projects with repositories;
  • the viva forms;
  • the AI-use logs sampled, with any integrity cases (outcome only);
  • the CLO attainment sheet (T12) and the course report with the corrective action.

Keep this for the period the department and the accreditation body require. That period must be checked; these plans do not state one.

6. Support for weaker students across the track

The intake is about 15% strong, 60% mid-level and 25% below average (framework section 1). The track needs stronger mathematics than the others (track overview). Each course file gives its own measures. These are shared.

Early detection.

  • CSE-3673 week 1: an unmarked Level 0 diagnostic on linear algebra, probability and Python (outline). Students below a set mark get the refresher sheets and are told plainly what the track demands, in time for the add/drop period.
  • Early-warning points in every course: after quiz 1, after practical test 1 and after the mid-term. A student below 40% at any of these points gets a meeting with the teacher and a written plan of two or three actions.

Mathematics bridging.

CourseMathematics the course usesBridging helpWhen

CSE-4783Percentages and rates, percentiles, a confidence interval for a difference in proportionsShort worked sheets; the weekly SQL clinic covers the arithmetic of totalsWeeks 2–3 and 10
CSE-4883Cost and capacity arithmetic, percentiles of latency, the population stability index, Cohen's kappa, metrics by group from confusion matrices, intervals for a proportionWorked "numbers sheets" for each; each lab has a checklist and a filled-in example document (outline)Weeks 4–12

In class and lab.

  • Starter notebooks and starter repositories with the structure in place (CSE-3673 outline), so that a weak student spends time on the decision, not on boilerplate.
  • One-page checklists for each recurring task: pipeline, training loop, star schema, release check.
  • Worked example first, then a faded example, then an independent task.
  • Mixed pairs in labs (a stronger with a weaker student), changed every few weeks. Not for the practical tests or the viva.
  • Sentence frames for every written deliverable (templates T7 and T9), because English writing is a second difficulty on top of the content (CSE-3673 outline).
  • A Bangla–English glossary of the course's key terms, built by the class during the semester.

Outside class.

  • Office hours of at least two hours a week for each teacher, published in week 1.
  • A weekly clinic (mathematics in CSE-3673 and CSE-4773, SQL in CSE-4783, labs in CSE-4883) run by the lab instructor or a trained senior student.
  • Peer tutoring by strong students from the previous cohort, where the department can recognise or pay for it. Peer tutors explain; they do not write students' code.
  • Recorded walkthroughs of each lab (needed anyway for CSE-4884 weeks 1–6).

Second chances that keep standards.

  • Weekly lab work: the best 10 of 11 sessions count (R1).
  • Quizzes: best three of four count (best two of three in CSE-4883), as the outlines set.
  • No change to the pass rules or the Level 0 assessments.

For the strong 15%. Each outline lists stretch goals. Each course file shows where in the semester a strong student can take one up, and how it is recognised: in the project rubric's "Strong" level, not with extra marks beyond 100.

7. Course teachers: shared responsibilities and qualification principles

Each course file gives the specific responsibilities and the proposed minimum qualification for its theory teacher and lab instructor. These principles apply to all four.

Responsibilities shared by all AID teachers.

  • Before the semester: run every lab yourself on a lab PC (and on the free notebook tier where used); prepare the datasets and systems in A10; publish the one-page tool list; set up the course repository organisation and the learning platform; agree dates with the other track teachers (section 8).
  • Every week: teach to the plan; mark weekly lab work within one week; check AI-use logs on a sample; keep the attendance and the lab record; run the clinic or office hours.
  • Assessment and moderation: set papers to the blueprint (5.1); moderate (5.2); return marked work with feedback within two weeks; complete the CLO attainment sheet (5.3).
  • AI-use rules: state the level of every task in writing; enforce Level 0 conditions; sample-check logs; handle breaches under the university's academic-integrity procedure.
  • Evidence: keep the course file (5.4).
  • Data ethics and privacy: approve every project dataset before work starts (licence, source, personal data); make sure partner data has its personal fields removed and stays on department storage; forbid scraping of private social-media content (CSE-4773 outline); where a project collects new data from people, follow the university's research-ethics procedure if one exists. That procedure must be checked; these plans do not assume one.
  • Coordination: with the other track courses and the capstone (section 8).

Qualification principles.

  • All qualifications in the course files are proposals for the department to confirm.
  • The University Grants Commission (UGC), the accreditation body (BAETE) and the university may have rules on who may teach or examine a course, and on the degree a teacher must hold. These plans do not cite any such rule. The department must check the current rules and apply them where they are stricter.
  • Framework section 16 allows industry adjuncts for new or heavily revised courses. Each course file says where one would help.
  • The lab instructor must have done every lab of the course before the semester, whatever their degree.

9. Track-wide issues for the committee

Each course file lists its own issues in section 9. These are the ones that affect more than one course, or the framework itself.

  1. The viva cannot fit in one 2-hour lab session. All four outlines hold the project demonstration and every individual viva in lab week 14. At about 10 minutes per student plus demonstrations, a lab section of 30 needs about five to six examiner-hours. The lesson plans assume two examiners and one extra 2-hour slot in week 14 or in the examination period. The department must timetable this, and must decide whether vivas may run outside the lab slot.
  2. No AI or API budget in the framework. Framework section 16 names purchases for the EMB, SEC and INF tracks, but not the hosted-API spending cap that CSE-4774 (week 12) and CSE-4884 rely on, nor a lab file server for cached model weights (CSE-4774). Both need a decision and a small budget before the first offering.
  3. Mid-term and final split. The framework leaves the split of the 80 written marks to the university regulation. These plans use 30/50 as a placeholder. The regulation must be confirmed before papers are set.
  4. Thresholds not set anywhere. The CLO attainment threshold (50% proposed), the course target (60% proposed), the viva "cannot explain" threshold, the contribution factor for pair projects and the make-up test rule are all proposals in this README. The department should set them once for the whole programme.
  5. The shared writing rubric does not exist yet. Framework section 12 and CSE-4783 refer to it. R5 is a stand-in.
  6. Semester-7 assessment congestion. The CSE-4774 and CSE-4784 outlines both put practical test 1 in week 7 and practical test 2 in week 13. Capstone Project I holds its proposal defence in week 7 and its prototype review with individual viva in week 13. Both labs hold their vivas in week 14. An AID student therefore has two Level 0 tests and a capstone milestone in week 7 and again in week 13, and two vivas in week 14. The weeks are fixed in the outlines, so any move is a committee decision.
  7. Semester-8 week 14. The Capstone Project II final defence, the Industry Practice vivas and the CSE-4884 production-readiness review and viva all fall in week 14, on two campus days (CSE-4883 file, section 9).

Course-specific issues are in section 9 of each course file.
