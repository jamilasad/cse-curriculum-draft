Credit and contact-hour rules

Framework section 4. Structure

  • Theory: 1 credit = 1 contact hour per week.
  • Paired lab: 1 credit = 2 contact hours per week; 1.5 credits = 3 hours. Three-hour labs are kept where set-up and debugging time matters most: Programming I and II, Data Structures and Digital Logic Design. The two lab-only communication courses, Developer Tools & Technical Communication Lab and Technical & Professional Communication, are also 1.5 credits for 3 hours.
  • Studio (lab-only project course): credit is earned by scheduled studio hours plus logged project hours, 25 hours per credit in total. Software Engineering Studio: 3 credits, 4 scheduled hours. AI Application & Agent Engineering Studio: 3 credits, 3 scheduled hours.
  • Capstone: 3 + 5 credits with 1 and 2 scheduled supervision hours.
  • Industry Practice: 4 credits for at least 200 logged hours; no scheduled class hours.
  • Core Competency Assessment: 1 credit, pass/fail, earned by passing the practical examination (section 8); preparation and the optional clinics make up the notional hours.
  • Semester length: outlines assume 14 teaching weeks.

The studio, capstone and practice rules depart from the "1 lab credit = 2 contact hours" rule. This is deliberate: project work does not need a scheduled room for every hour, and scheduled hours are what cost students travel. The basis is the accreditation body's own definition of a laboratory credit: 60 notional hours of student work, of which at least 25 are spent in laboratory, studio, project or dissertation work. Studios and the capstone meet the 25-hour minimum with scheduled plus logged supervised hours. Industry Practice asks for 200 logged hours for 4 credits (50 per credit), above the minimum and within the 60-hour notional workload.

Semester limits, checked against the data

At most 24 contact hours a week (Decision 9) and at most five credit-bearing theory courses a semester, GED included (Decision 8). Total 140–145 credits including GED (Decision 6); the draft has 142.
