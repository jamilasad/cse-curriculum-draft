Summary

The department's earlier competitive-programming plan has a sound goal: graduates who can solve unfamiliar coding problems quickly and correctly, because that is what Bangladeshi employers test. Flow B keeps that goal and most of the practice volume. It rejects the two parts that no longer work: Codeforces rating targets, and counts of solved problems used as evidence. Since 2025, AI assistants solve contest problems at world-final level, so neither ratings nor counts show what a student can do alone. In their place, Flow B awards levels only in supervised contests on the department's own judge. On entrepreneurship, Flow B gives every student 6 credits and offers an opt-in venture route that can use up to 11 more (capstone and Industry Practice). The design aims at businesses that Bangladeshi graduates can actually start and run.

1.2 C first: reject

Draft 1's reason for Python first was that the weaker 85% of the intake gain more from early success in problem solving than from early exposure to memory management. That still holds. Flow B adds a second reason that Draft 1 missed.

Draft 1 says the department "can choose C first ... without changing anything else". That is not correct. The first-year mathematics courses use Python from semester 1:

  • CSE-1123 Discrete Mathematics checks its exercises with short Python programs.
  • MATH-1107 Calculus I has a Python assignment.
  • MATH-1209 Linear Algebra & Numerical Computing (S2) lists CSE-1121 (Python) as a prerequisite and has four NumPy notebook assignments.
  • STAT-2313 (S3) and CSE-3545 AI & Machine Learning (S5) also assume Python.

With C first, the department has two options. It can teach Python as a third language in year 1, or it can remove the computing work from four mathematics courses. Both cost more than the swap gains.

What C first was meant to achieve is also reached in Flow B. Students write C from semester 2. They practise contest-style problems in C in CSE-1232 that same semester. They move to C++ with the standard library in CSE-1232 and in Data Structures.

I found no strong evidence that the first language decides outcomes on its own. Teaching method matters more: tracing, testing and supervised practice. The choice therefore turns on how well each language fits the rest of the plan, and Python fits better.

If the department still prefers C first, these outlines must change: CSE-1121, CSE-1221, CSE-1123, MATH-1107, MATH-1209 and STAT-2313. The CSE-3545 prerequisite must change too. Draft 1's C course would also lose its opening weeks, where students port Python programs they already know into C.

1.3 CP 1 in semester 2: adapt as CSE-1232 Problem Solving Lab

Background. In the 2022 syllabus, Competitive Programming 1 (CSE-1230) was already a 1-credit, 2-hour course in semester 2, and Competitive Programming 2 (CSE-2430) was in semester 4. The department's earlier plan keeps CP 1 in semester 2 and moves CP 2 to semester 5.

What the department's plan gets right. Problem solving is the skill Bangladeshi employers screen hardest for fresh graduates (section 2.2). Draft 1 starts judged practice only in semester 3, so the semester in which students first become fluent in a second language holds no problem-solving practice at all. Regular, supervised practice with quick feedback also helps the middle 60% more than any other group. They do not organise such practice for themselves.

What Flow B changes.

  • The course is a 1-credit lab (2 hours a week), not a contest course.
  • Each week holds a short supervised contest on the department's judge at AI Level 0, followed by upsolving and explanation.
  • Codeforces 800–1100 is used as a guide for choosing problems, not as a target.
  • Marks come only from supervised work.
  • Draft 1's framing is kept: model the problem first, then code. Speed is practised as working within a time box, not ranked.

Two facts from section 2 justify some timed practice: Enosis's screening test lasts 80 minutes, and Therap's is a 2-hour paper test. Draft 1's principle "repetition for speed is no longer a goal" is therefore softened, not dropped.

Cost: +1 credit; semester 2 rises from 22 to 24 hours a week. A cheaper variant is in open question 3.

1.4 Problem quotas in Data Structures and Algorithms: adopt the volume, change what counts

Draft 1's labs already set roughly as many problems as the quotas ask for. The Data Structures lab sets 12 weekly sets with 3 core problems each (36), plus standard and challenge tiers. The Algorithms lab sets about 33 core problems and roughly 65–75 in all. So the disagreement is not about volume. It is about what counts as evidence.

A count of accepted submissions on LeetCode or Codeforces no longer shows that the student solved anything. AI assistants solve these problems at sight (section 2.1).

Flow B keeps the quotas as a published practice plan:

  • 50 problems in Data Structures and 60 in Algorithms, each list arranged by pattern.
  • 30 core problems in each course, required for the weekly check-off.
  • A mix of interview format (write a function) and contest format (read input, print output).
  • Department-written variants hosted on the judge, with public equivalents listed for extra practice.

A core problem counts only when it is accepted on the judge and the student can explain it at the bench if it is drawn at random. Marks come from the two supervised practical tests, which sample the same patterns with changed details.

1.5 CP 2 in semester 5: reject as a course, keep the aim

It does not fit. Semester 5 already has 23 contact hours. A 1-credit lab adds 2, which breaks the 24-hour limit. Semester 6 has the same problem.

It is also too early. Students apply for Industry Practice placements in weeks 10–12 of semester 7 and look for jobs in semester 8. Interview practice in semester 5 would be about 18 months stale by then.

Interview formats are changing. Some large employers now hold one round in person, and some have added rounds where the candidate works with an AI assistant (section 2.1). A course built around "50 more LeetCode problems" prepares for a format that is in flux.

What Flow B does instead, at no extra credit:

  • CSE-4760 (S7) gains a coding-interview strand in three weeks: timed problems solved aloud, an AI-assisted round (read, fix and extend unfamiliar code with an assistant, then explain how it was checked), and a short coding question in the mock-interview viva. CSE-4760 makes room by moving its contracts-and-payment week to CSE-4753.
  • Interview season (S7–S8, opt-in, no credit). The department's CP 2 list becomes a curated list of 50 interview problems, with two supervised mock contests and online mock interviews with alumni.
  • The verified problem-solving record (section 1.6). It gives students evidence to show employers.

A variant with a separate 1-credit interview lab in semester 8 is costed in open question 4.

1.6 Rating targets: reject, and use verified levels instead

Reasons to reject them (evidence in sections 2.1 and 2.3):

  1. A rating no longer shows unaided skill. AI systems solved 10 to 12 of the 12 problems at the 2025 ICPC World Finals. Codeforces had to write a rule against AI use in rated rounds (September 2024), and its community still reports AI cheating in 2025–2026. A grade tied to a rating rewards breaking that rule.
  2. The targets are out of reach for most of the intake. Even among self-selected active Codeforces users, only about a quarter to a third are rated 1400 or more. Our intake is not self-selected: 60% are mid-level and 25% are below average.
  3. A rating is relative and depends on conditions outside the course. It moves with the global field. It needs live evening contests, which usually start at about 20:35 Bangladesh time, plus a laptop and a stable connection at home. A course grade should measure the student against a fixed standard.
  4. Employers do not ask for a rating. No Bangladeshi employer in the evidence asks for a rating cut-off. They set their own tests (section 2.2).

What Flow B offers instead: the verified problem-solving record. The department certifies levels only in supervised sittings at Level 0. These are the CSE-1232 contests and tests, the practical tests in the Data Structures and Algorithms labs, and a monthly open supervised contest that any student may sit. Problems are new or changed, are written by the department, and are calibrated against Codeforces problems of the same type.

1.7 Where Flow B agrees and disagrees with Draft 1

Agrees:

  • Problem solving means modelling and choosing a technique, not speed for its own sake.
  • Marks come from supervised work.
  • Contest training stays co-curricular for those who want it.
  • The CSE-2440 floor and gate are unchanged.

Disagrees:

  • Draft 1 left semester 2 without judged practice. Flow B adds CSE-1232.
  • Draft 1 gave timed fluency too little weight, given how Bangladeshi employers test.
  • Draft 1 had no way for a student to show problem-solving skill to an employer. The verified record adds one.
  • Draft 1's claim that C first is a free swap is wrong (section 1.2).

1.8 Strong objections, and a better way to the same goal

  1. Do not grade or require Codeforces ratings.
    • Reason: a rating can be earned with AI help in breach of Codeforces's own rule, is out of reach for most of the intake, and is relative.
    • Better way: the department-certified levels P1–P5 (section 1.6). Strong students can still chase ratings in the club, under Codeforces's rules.
  2. Do not count solved problems as evidence.
    • Reason: an assistant produces accepted solutions to public problems at once. In a February 2024 experiment, ChatGPT-assisted candidates passed interviews built on verbatim LeetCode problems 73% of the time, and interviewers did not notice.
    • Better way: publish the problem lists as a practice plan. Count a problem only when the student can explain it. Give marks for supervised tests that use department-written variants.
  3. Do not put CP 2 in semester 5.
    • Reason: it breaks the 24-hour limit, and interviews come 18 months later.
    • Better way: the semester-7 interview strand, the opt-in interview season and the verified record (section 1.5).
  4. Do not switch to C first without a plan for Python.
    • Reason: at least six outlines would have to change, because the first-year mathematics courses use Python from semester 1 (section 1.2).
    • Better way: keep Python first and start contest-style practice in C in semester 2.
  5. Do not give every student 10 credits of entrepreneurship.
    • Reason: in most studies, classroom entrepreneurship teaching changes students' intentions little or not at all, and in one well-known study it lowered them (section 2.6). Bangladesh's start-up funding fell to about USD 6 million in the first half of 2026. Most graduates will be employees, freelancers or members of service firms.
    • Better way: 6 credits for everyone, aimed at skills that help in all four careers (section 3.1), plus an opt-in venture route for the few who start something.

2. The judgement criteria

The evidence below was checked on 24 November 2026. Each point gives its date and source. "Secondary" means I read a report about the source, not the source itself.

2.1 How coding interviews are run now that AI can solve rated problems

What AI can do.

  • Contest problems. At the ICPC World Finals in September 2025, OpenAI reported that its system solved all 12 problems. Google DeepMind reported that Gemini 2.5 Deep Think solved 10. Both results are company-reported and were not official ICPC rankings (Google DeepMind, 17 Sep 2025; The Decoder, Sep 2025). Any problem rated 800–1500 is therefore within easy reach of a free assistant.
  • Interview problems. In an interviewing.io experiment published in February 2024, candidates secretly using ChatGPT passed interviews built on verbatim LeetCode problems 73% of the time. They passed modified problems 67% of the time, against 53% for candidates working alone. No interviewer detected the help. Custom problems reduced the advantage (interviewing.io; Business Today, 22 Feb 2024). Today's models are much stronger than the ones used then.
  • Codeforces. Codeforces restricted AI use in rated rounds on 14 September 2024 (rule). Community posts in 2025–2026 report mass AI cheating and propose stronger controls (example 1, example 2). Announcements in November 2026 still remind contestants of the rule.

How employers responded. There are three responses, often in the same company.

  1. More in-person or proctored rounds.
    • Google's CEO said on the Lex Fridman podcast (June 2025) that Google would hold at least one in-person round "to make sure the fundamentals are there". This was widely reported in August 2025 (secondary: Entrepreneur, Deccan Herald).
    • Amazon's recruiting guidelines, reported in February 2025, allow candidates who use AI during interviews to be disqualified (secondary: ITPro).
    • A Gartner survey of 3,000 candidates (2Q 2025) found that 6% admitted to interview fraud. Gartner predicts that by 2028 one in four candidate profiles worldwide will be fake (Gartner, 31 Jul 2025).
    • One interview-security vendor, Fabric, says it flagged 38.5% of 19,368 live interviews for AI-assisted cheating between July 2025 and January 2026. This is a single vendor's data, so treat it with caution (summary).
  2. Rounds where AI is allowed and the candidate is judged on how they use it.
    • Meta began piloting an "AI-enabled" coding interview in July 2025. 404 Media reported it first, and Meta confirmed it to Business Insider (404 Media; HR Grapevine, 1 Aug 2025). Secondary accounts say candidates are judged on problem solving, code quality, verification and communication, and that a classic round without AI is kept beside it.
    • In 2026, Google is reported to be piloting a "code comprehension" round for junior and mid-level roles in some US teams. Candidates read, debug and improve an existing codebase with Gemini available. I could confirm this only through secondary career sites (University of Miami career blog, 14 May 2026; ByteHerald, Oct 2026), not through a Google source.
  3. Custom questions in place of well-known public ones (interviewing.io's advice above).

What this means for the curriculum.

  • Unaided fluency is still tested, in at least one supervised round. A student must be able to solve a medium problem alone, on paper or a plain editor, and explain it.
  • A new skill is tested beside it: reading unfamiliar code, steering an assistant, and showing how the result was checked.
  • Online ratings and solved counts have lost their value as signals, which pushes employers toward their own tests.

Flow B trains both skills and gives students a signal that cannot be faked: the supervised record.

Two related findings:

  • The project's own research file (bd-software-companies.md, 17 November 2026) notes that captive development centres hire through problem-solving and data-structures tests, and that bank and government software firms value domain knowledge.
  • Entry-level hiring is shrinking. A report dated 3 September 2026 says entry-level hiring fell by about 50% at Dream71 and by about 70% at Tiger IT, as firms use AI for routine work. This is a secondary source; I did not find the companies' own statements (newskarnataka, 3 Sep 2026). The Daily Star also reports that the outlook is most uncertain for entry-level developers (Daily Star, 2026).

What this means for the curriculum.

  • The common screen is two or three problems of roughly LeetCode easy-to-medium level, solved within a time limit, often on paper. This is about P2–P3 in Flow B's terms.
  • SQL is tested almost everywhere.
  • English and explanation matter in the interview.
  • Some captive centres value contest experience, but none in this evidence asks for a Codeforces rating.

So the department's instinct is right that problem solving decides who gets the scarce entry-level jobs. The skill must be shown on the employer's test, not on a rating page.

2.3 Are rating targets of 1300–1500 by semester 5 realistic for the middle 60%?

The data.

  • A 2026 analysis of Codeforces data puts most users between 800 and 1400. Among users active in the last six months, about 28% appear to be at 1400 or above (Codeforces blog 151862, 2026; the share is my reading of its "Top %" column).
  • A 2024 comment gives a median rating of 1143 and a mean of 1205. This is unverified (blog 131886).
  • I found no published median time to reach 1400. Individual accounts describe 12–19 months of steady practice, and many describe long stalls at Pupil (below 1400) (example).

My judgement.

  • These figures describe volunteers who chose to compete, and some of their ratings are inflated by AI cheating.
  • An unselected intake of 200–300, where 60% are mid-level and 25% below average, will do worse than that self-selected population.
  • A target of 1300–1500 by semester 5 is realistic for most of the strong 15% and for part of the middle group. It is not realistic for the middle 60% as a whole.
  • Set as a requirement, the target would either fail most students or push them to cheat.

The P-levels in section 1.6 put the department's bands where they fit: P1 for everyone, P2 as the floor, P3 as the standard most of the middle can reach, and P4–P5 as stretch goals.

2.4 Assessing practice honestly when AI is available

Flow B uses the same rules in CSE-1232, CSE-2322, CSE-2422 and the open contests.

  1. Marks come only from supervised work at Level 0. These are the weekly contests in the lab, the practical tests and a paper round. Take-home practice earns marks only through a random bench explanation, as Draft 1 already does.
  2. No verbatim public problems in assessed sittings. Assistants have seen them. The department writes variants in local settings with changed details and keeps them in a bank, as for CSE-2440: families of problems, several variants each, retired after use.
  3. The lab is locked down. The judge (DOMjudge or the department's own) runs on the lab network only. There is no internet, no USB storage and no AI extensions. The CSE-2440 exam image is reused.
  4. A paper round each semester. Students trace a program and hand-write two solutions. Some employers test on paper (section 2.2).
  5. Explain or it does not count. After each contest, a teaching assistant asks a random sample of students to explain one accepted solution. A student who cannot explain a solution loses that problem's marks.
  6. Similarity checks. JPlag runs on all contest submissions, and flagged pairs go to an oral.
  7. Online rated contests are never graded. The club supports them under Codeforces's AI rule. A club member caught cheating loses club support.
  8. AI-assisted practice is taught openly, later. From semester 4 (the Algorithms project) and in semester 7 (CSE-4760), students practise the AI-assisted interview format at Level 2. The skill is assessed by asking them to explain what they checked.

Scale. At 300 students and 30 seats a lab, the weekly CSE-1232 contest runs as 10 lab sessions of 2 hours. That is the same load as any 1-credit lab with ten sections. The problem bank needs about 70 new or changed problems a semester for CSE-1232. Three or four teachers, with paid senior students from the strong 15% who no longer take the course, can write them.

2.6 Evidence behind the entrepreneurship design

Effect of teaching entrepreneurship.

  • A Dutch evaluation of a student mini-company programme found no effect on self-assessed entrepreneurial skills and a negative effect on students' intention to start a business. The authors were Oosterbeek, van Praag and IJsselstein, European Economic Review 54(3), 2010 (EconPapers).
  • A 2022 meta-analysis reports a positive association between entrepreneurship education and intention, and finds that national context moderates it (Frontiers in Psychology, 2022).
  • Effects on actual venture creation are less well established. I did not find a strong study for South Asia.
  • Conclusion: keep the compulsory part small and practical, and put the depth into an opt-in route where students build something real.

The Bangladesh venture market.

  • Funding is thin. Start-up funding in 2025 was about USD 124 million across 12 deals, and one USD 110 million acquisition made up most of it. In the first half of 2026, funding fell about 95% year on year, to about USD 6 million across 6 deals (LightCastle / Startup Bangladesh, Year in Review 2025; H1 2026 report; Dhaka Tribune). Graduates should plan to start a business that earns from its own customers, not one that waits for investment.
  • Service and freelance work is large but changing. Bangladesh supplies about 16% of the world's online freelance labour, but business spending on freelance marketplaces has fallen sharply. AI is taking template web work, while AI-integration work is growing (project file cse-claims-evidence.md, citing LightCastle, August 2026, and the Daily Star, September 2026).
  • Bangladeshi teams already sell software products worldwide, for example WordPress plugin firms such as weDevs, WPDeveloper and Ollyo. Only Ollyo was checked in the project's research file.
  • Payments are a practical barrier. As of September 2026, PayPal accounts cannot be opened with a Bangladeshi address. Bangladesh Bank issued a cross-border digital payment framework on 29 July 2026, with no start date (cse-career-tracks.md).

3. Entrepreneurship: design of the 6–10 credits

3.1 What entrepreneurship should mean in this programme

The programme's PO12 already defines it broadly: "the competences necessary to transform opportunities and ideas into value". For a Bangladeshi CSE graduate in 2027–2031 that means four routes:

  1. A software service firm for foreign or local clients: the main business model of the country's industry.
  2. Freelancer to agency: from selling one's own hours to running a small team with a brand, contracts and repeat clients.
  3. A product sold worldwide or locally, often bootstrapped: a SaaS tool, a plugin or app, or a B2B tool for Bangladeshi small businesses, schools, clinics or farms.
  4. The intrapreneur: an employee who finds a problem, makes a case for it and builds the solution inside a firm. Most graduates will take this route.

The compulsory credits teach what all four routes share. The venture route is for students who want to try route 1, 2 or 3 for real while still at university.

3.4 Links to PO12, the capstone, Industry Practice and CSE-4753

PO12. Draft 1 assesses PO12 with a CLO in only one course, CSE-4753 (the website build notes flag this). Flow B makes CSE-3657 and CSE-4753 the main evidence and keeps Capstone II's continuation section as support. That gives three assessed places.

Capstone venture option ("V route").

  • Who qualifies:
    • The team passed CSE-3657 with a validated concept: at least 15 recorded customer interviews and one experiment result that supports the problem.
    • An outside mentor agrees to act as the problem owner, in place of a client. This can be an alumni founder, an incubator mentor or a partner-firm manager.
    • The team, its supervisor and the university sign a written note on who owns the work. This depends on the university's intellectual property policy (open question 7).
  • How many: in the first two cohorts, no more than about 15% of teams.
  • What stays the same: teams, milestones, marks, AI rules and the complexity declaration (WP1 and three of WP2–WP7). A landing page does not pass.
  • What changes:
    • The "client" is the target customer group, and stakeholders (WP6) are customer segments plus the mentor.
    • The final report adds a venture-evidence chapter: interviews, usage, pilots or revenue.
    • Marks go to engineering quality and to decisions based on evidence. Commercial success is never marked.
    • A venture that fails its customer tests may pivot once by the design review (M2). After that it becomes an ordinary capstone on the same product, with the mentor as client.

Industry Practice Mode E: own venture.

  • Who qualifies: a student whose venture passed the CSE-3657 pitch review or is a capstone venture, with an outside mentor who confirms the logbook weekly.
  • What counts: customer interviews, sales, partnerships, operations and engineering outside the capstone's scope. No hour is counted twice with the capstone, a rule that already exists.
  • Hurdle by week 13: at least one outside validation, such as:
    • a paying customer;
    • a signed pilot or letter of intent from a named organisation;
    • acceptance into a recognised incubator or accelerator;
    • a public release with logged active use over four weeks.
  • If the venture stops: the student moves to Mode C or D for the remaining hours, as Draft 1 already allows when a placement collapses.
  • How many: plan for 5–10% of the cohort at first.
  • A founder or early employee role in someone else's start-up already counts under Mode A or B.

CSE-4753. It keeps its project-management and economics weeks.

  • Moved out to CSE-3657: the lean canvas and pitch.
  • Moved in from CSE-4760: contracts, statements of work, invoices, lawful foreign payment and tax, so that the "business of software" sits in one course.
  • Its venture mini-project becomes a costed business case for continuing the capstone product. That case feeds Capstone II's continuation section.

3.5 Making it realistic for Bangladesh

CSE-3657 teaches these points explicitly:

  • Revenue first. With investment funding at about USD 6 million in half a year, every concept must show how it could earn from customers within 12 months. Unit economics include the cloud and AI cost per user, using CSE-4753's methods.
  • Selling abroad from Bangladesh: marketplaces, plugin directories and app stores; direct B2B sales to foreign firms; what PayPal's absence means; lawful channels for receiving export earnings. Rules are taught "described generally, check the current rule", because they change.
  • Selling at home: relationship-based B2B sales, tenders, the long payment cycles of institutions, and mobile financial services as a payment channel.
  • What AI changes: template web work is losing value; integration, automation and domain-specific tools are gaining. An MVP is now cheap to build, so the hard part is finding a paying customer, not writing the code.
  • Legal basics, described generally: sole proprietorship, partnership and private limited company (registered with RJSC); trade licence; tax identification; a founders' agreement with vesting; IP assignment; open-source licences. Legal detail is coordinated with CSE-4805.

3.6 What the department needs

  • Teachers. One CSE teacher with industry experience leads CSE-3657, co-teaching with a business-faculty member, if the university's business school can release one. Alumni founders join online.
  • Mentors. About 60–75 teams take CSE-3657 at an intake of 300. A pool of about 15 mentors, each meeting four or five teams online once a fortnight, covers it. External judges are needed for pitch day.
  • Links to outside programmes. A memorandum with one incubator or accelerator gives venture-route students a hurdle and mentors. The university's own incubator, if any, counts. National programmes such as Startup Bangladesh are also options; check their current status.
  • A small optional fund for cloud credits and customer-test costs (a few thousand taka per venture team).
  • An IP policy for student ventures, settled before the first venture capstone.

3.7 Variant: 8 credits for everyone

If the department wants 8 compulsory credits, the natural addition is a 2-credit Venture Studio for all students. To stay at 145, it must be paid for. The cheapest option is to remove the Track IV labs (−1) and cut both the Digital Logic Design lab and CSE-1130 from 1.5 to 1 credit (−0.5 each). Placement:

  • In semester 8, the hours fit: 13 − 2 + 4 = 15.
  • In semester 7, it would break the 24-hour limit (22 + 4 = 26) unless the studio uses the pending total-hours basis.

I do not recommend this variant. The evidence in section 2.6 does not support compulsory depth, and the hands-on hours it removes are among the most useful in the programme.

6. Course descriptions for new and changed courses

Each description covers aims, content, weekly rhythm, assessment and the AI-use rule. Unchanged parts of Draft 1's outlines stand.

CSE-1232 Problem Solving Lab (new; S2; lab, 1 credit, 2 h/week)

  • Aims. Turn short word problems into correct, efficient programs, unaided. Build the habits of reading the limits, working small cases by hand, writing a brute force first, testing edge cases, then improving.
  • Code. CSE-1232 is a new code, following Draft 1's rule not to reuse an old code for a different course. The 2022 CP 1 was CSE-1230 (S2, 1 credit, 2 hours). If the department sees this as the same course revised, it may keep CSE-1230.
  • Prerequisites. CSE-1121; taken with CSE-1221. Recommended, not required, for CSE-2321.
  • Problem list. A published list of 60 practice problems, 30 of them core.
  • Content.
    • P1: implementation, loops and arrays, strings, divisibility and gcd, simulation.
    • P2: sorting and custom order, counting, prefix sums, two pointers, greedy checked by searching for a counterexample, binary search on a sorted array.
    • From week 9, C++ "as C with a library" (vector, sort, map, set), as the bridge to Data Structures.
    • Problems are department-written in local settings. Codeforces 800–1100 and LeetCode Easy problems are listed for extra practice.
    • Contests accept C, C++ or Python. Teaching examples are in C.
  • Weekly rhythm.
    • 15 minutes: one technique on paper.
    • 60 minutes: a supervised contest of three problems (floor, standard, stretch) on the local judge.
    • 30–45 minutes: upsolving and explanations, with random bench checks by teaching assistants.
    • About 5 practice problems a week at home.
  • Assessment (100). Weekly contests, best 10 of 12: 30. Two supervised tests (weeks 7 and 13, 90 minutes): 30. Paper round: 15. Practice record and bench explanations: 10. Viva: 15. P1 is the pass floor; P2 can be certified here.
  • AI use. Level 0 in every contest, test, paper round and the viva. Level 1 for home practice: an assistant may explain a concept or an error, not write a solution. Students are told plainly that marks come only from supervised work.

CSE-2322 Data Structures Lab (changed: content only)

  • What changes. The weekly problem sets become a published 50-problem ladder by pattern: hashing (8), two pointers and sliding window (6), stacks and queues (6), linked lists (5), trees and binary search trees (8), heaps (5), BFS and DFS (8), mixed (4).
    • 30 core problems are required for weekly check-off; 15 are standard and 5 or more are challenge (roughly Codeforces 1100–1300).
    • Problems come in both formats (write a function; read input and print output).
  • Practical tests. The tests in weeks 7 and 13 certify P2, the course floor.
  • Unchanged. Everything else in Draft 1 stands: structures built by hand, the AI-review exercises, the project and the viva.
  • AI use. As in Draft 1.

CSE-2422 Algorithms Lab (changed: content only)

  • What changes. The judged sets become a 60-problem ladder: binary search (6), sorting and greedy (8), divide and conquer (4), dynamic programming (14), graphs — shortest paths, topological order, MST, union–find (12), strings (4), backtracking (4), intervals (4), bits and number theory (4).
    • 30 are core, 20 standard and 10 challenge (roughly Codeforces 1200–1500).
    • Practical tests offer P3 (standard tier). The pass floor stays at Draft 1's minimum competency.
    • One exercise near the project mirrors the AI-assisted interview: fix and extend an unfamiliar algorithmic module with an assistant (Level 2), then explain the checks at Level 0.
  • AI use. Otherwise as in Draft 1.

CSE-2440 Core Competency Assessment (small change)

  • Unchanged: stations, pass rules, timing and the gate.
  • Changed:
    • CSE-1232 joins the feeder courses and the question-bank committee.
    • The judge harness and exam image are shared with the CSE-1232 contests.
    • Results on stations 1 and 3 are also reported against the P-levels, for feedback only. The CCA stays the floor; the P-levels recognise work above it.

CSE-3657 Technology Entrepreneurship & Innovation (new; S6; theory, 3 credits, 3 h/week)

  • Aims. Find a real problem worth solving, test it with customers, and design a business that can pay for itself in Bangladesh's conditions. These skills also make graduates better employees.
  • Prerequisites. CSE-2433 and CSE-2434; taken with CSE-3660.
  • Content (weeks 1–14).
    1. Four routes into business (section 3.1); the Bangladeshi funding reality; forming teams.
    2. Finding problems: observation and the "jobs to be done" idea; local sectors and global niches.
    3. Customer interviews I: designing them, sampling, avoiding leading questions, consent.
    4. Customer interviews II: making sense of the notes; deciding whether there is enough evidence of a problem.
    5. Value proposition; competitors and substitutes; AI as a feature and as a way to build.
    6. Minimum viable product and experiments: landing page, concierge, "Wizard of Oz"; measures.
    7. Evidence review; mid-term.
    8. Business models: subscription, licence, freemium, marketplace, fixed-price and retainer services, freelance agency.
    9. Unit economics: acquisition cost, lifetime value, churn, gross margin with cloud and AI cost per user.
    10. Selling at home and abroad: B2B sales, tenders, marketplaces and directories, payments.
    11. Legal forms and founders' agreements; IP and licences (described generally).
    12. Funding: bootstrapping, grants and competitions, incubators, angel and venture investment, dilution.
    13. Writing the business case; ethics of products (dark patterns, data use).
    14. Pitch day with outside judges; the venture-option decision.
  • Weekly rhythm. One hour of input, then a two-hour workshop on the team's own venture. Teams of 4–5 do at least two hours of fieldwork a week outside class.
  • Assessment. University rule: written mid-term and final 80, continuous assessment 20.
    • Exams: case work — analyse interview notes, find untested assumptions, compute unit economics, choose a model and a price, critique a pitch. Recall is no more than a fifth.
    • Continuous assessment: venture evidence file 10, pitch 5, quizzes 5. This departs from Draft 1's proposed 10/10 split (open question 9).
  • AI use. Level 0 for exams, quizzes and pitch questions. Level 2 with disclosure for venture work: drafting canvases, landing pages and analysis. Interviews must be real and on file for audit; inventing one is misconduct.

CSE-3660 AI Application & Agent Engineering Studio (small change)
