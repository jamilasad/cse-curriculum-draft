Research date: 17 November 2026. Refresh due: 17 May 2027.

This page shows the evidence used to design the proposed CSE curriculum. It summarises three research notes written in Bangla on 17 November 2026: one tests three common claims about CSE, one looks at career paths, and one describes what Bangladeshi software firms do. Each claim below gives its source and a confidence level. Job-market evidence ages fast, so the figures here should be checked again by the refresh date.

Why this curriculum

Many CSE graduates in Bangladesh have waited about a year for their first job. Employers say a new hire needs 6 to 12 months before doing useful work. Students and studies say teaching is too theoretical, with too little practice. Each year an estimated 12,000 to 20,000 CSE graduates compete for about 5,000 to 8,000 entry-level jobs. AI tools now do some routine coding and simple website work, and there are fewer junior jobs. Demand is still high for skills in AI, security, data, cloud and infrastructure. This curriculum keeps the core subjects, adds more hands-on work, and checks that every student can explain their own work. In the final years each student chooses one of five tracks, and some tracks suit students who are not at the top of the class.

Summary of findings

The job market for fresh graduates

Bangladesh produces more CSE graduates than there are entry-level jobs. Most studies say graduates lack practical skills, but one study disagrees.

  • Claim: Bangladesh produces an estimated 12,000–20,000 CSE graduates a year, but has only about 5,000–8,000 entry-level posts.
    Source: LightCastle, August 2026; DSi, date not given.
    Confidence: Low. Estimates from different sources, not official statistics.
  • Claim: An ADB tracer study of CSE and IT graduates from nine universities found that teaching was mostly theoretical, and many graduates waited about a year for a job. It recommended major syllabus revision, more hands-on practice, soft skills and industry exposure for teachers.
    Source: ADB, survey 2018, published 2019; Daily Star, date not given.
    Confidence: Medium. A survey across nine universities, but the data is from 2018.
  • Claim: Students at DU, BUET and NSU called the syllabus "outdated" and "stuck in the past"; one said the whole system centres on CGPA and projects are weakly assessed. Industry estimates that new hires need 6–12 months before they contribute.
    Source: Daily Star, 2 July 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. Opinions reported in one news article, not a survey.
  • Claim: The same report quotes BASIS: about 40% of posts in the IT sector are vacant while graduates are unemployed. This points to a mismatch of skills, not of numbers.
    Source: Daily Star, 2 July 2026 (quoting BASIS).
    Confidence: Low. The original BASIS document was not checked.
  • Claim: A peer-reviewed study quoted in the same report found that most companies ask for 2–3 years of experience, even for entry-level posts.
    Source: Daily Star, 2 July 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. One study, known only through a news report.
  • Claim: A Daffodil International University study gave local graduates an employability score of 2.72 out of 4, below what industry expects.
    Source: DigiBanglaTech, study dated August 2025.
    Confidence: Low. The report does not give the sample size or method.
  • Claim: Alam et al. surveyed 1,320 graduates and 32 stakeholders. They found a significant mismatch in advanced technology and digital skills.
    Source: Library record, 2025.
    Confidence: Medium. One survey with a stated sample size.
  • Claim: Counter-evidence: a study in Cogent Social Sciences found that graduates' ICT skills are adequate.
    Source: DOAJ, December 2024.
    Confidence: Medium. A single study that disagrees with the others. On both sides, most evidence is surveys, interviews and opinions.

AI and hiring

Most hard data on AI and hiring comes from the US. It shows fewer junior jobs, while senior and AI-related jobs grow.

  • Claim: In jobs most exposed to AI, employment of people aged 22–25 is 19% below the expected level, but there is no such gap for experienced workers. The cause is fewer new hires, not layoffs.
    Source: Stanford, August 2026 edition.
    Confidence: Medium. The authors call it early descriptive evidence, not proof of cause.
  • Claim: In the first quarter of 2026, only 4.5% of software job postings on Indeed were entry-level, against 46% across all sectors.
    Source: MASEconomics, date not given.
    Confidence: Medium. A secondary analysis of Indeed data, not Indeed's own report.
  • Claim: US companies hired about 113,000 software engineers from May 2025 to April 2026. At the 2022 peak they hired 239,000.
    Source: Lenny's Newsletter, 10 November 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. One data source, reported in a newsletter.
  • Claim: New York Fed data for 2024 shows recent-graduate unemployment of 7.8% in computer engineering and 7.0% in computer science, against 4.2% for all subjects. But underemployment was below 20%, against 42% on average: finding a job takes longer, but the job usually fits the degree.
    Source: VnExpress, date not given (2024 data).
    Confidence: Medium. Official data, reported through a news site.
  • Claim: Since February 2025, US software job postings rose about 15% while all postings fell 7%, but they are still 27.5% below the pre-pandemic level. Of the growth, 71% was in senior roles and 37% in roles with "AI" in the title.
    Source: Indeed Hiring Lab, 8 July 2026.
    Confidence: High. Direct data from the job site, but US only.
  • Claim: US projections for 2024–34: software developers, QA analysts and testers +15%; information security analysts +29%; computer programmers −6%.
    Source: BLS, projection period 2024–34.
    Confidence: Medium. Official projection, but made before or during fast AI progress.
  • Claim: The World Economic Forum lists big data specialists, fintech engineers, AI/ML specialists, and software and application developers among the fastest-growing jobs.
    Source: WEF Future of Jobs 2025, 2025.
    Confidence: Medium. A forecast; forecasts made during fast AI change are uncertain.
  • Claim: Taken together, demand is falling for people who only write code (programmers −6%). Demand is forecast to grow for people who understand a problem and build a system.
    Source: Research notes (author's synthesis), 17 November 2026; BLS, projection period 2024–34.
    Confidence: Low. Author's synthesis, not a published finding, and the forecasts are uncertain.

Remote work

Remote work still pays far more than local jobs, but there are fewer openings than in 2022, and most need experience. The notes conclude that remote work is a weak reason for a fresher to choose CSE, but a fair goal after 3–5 years of experience.

  • Claim: Entry-level developers in Bangladesh earn BDT 15,000–35,000 a month (about USD 136–318); in India they earn USD 360–540. So a remote job paying USD 1,000 a month would pay 3–7 times local entry pay.
    Source: Daily Star, 2 July 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. Salary figures from one news report. The "3–7 times" is only an arithmetic example; no reliable survey of what Bangladeshi remote developers really earn was found.
  • Claim: Bangladesh is the second-largest supplier of online labour in the Oxford Internet Institute index, with about 16% share. But it is behind in hourly wages.
    Source: Daily Star, date not given; Prothom Alo, date not given.
    Confidence: Medium. Index results reported through news articles.
  • Claim: Remote roles fell from 25% of open engineering roles at the end of 2022 to 14% now, a record low (TrueUp data). The number depends on the definition: by Indeed's count of software posts that mention remote or hybrid work, the fall is from 33.7% to 30.8%.
    Source: Lenny's Newsletter, 10 November 2026; Noon, date not given.
    Confidence: Medium. The direction is clear, but the size depends on the definition.
  • Claim: Freelance marketplaces' share of business spending fell from 0.66% in the last quarter of 2021 to 0.14% in the third quarter of 2025. Over the same time, spending on AI model providers rose from almost zero to 3%, and more than half of the earlier active buyers left.
    Source: Ramp Economics Lab data, via LightCastle, August 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. Spending data reported second-hand.
  • Claim: AI tools such as Lovable and Bolt now take simple website-building work. Some Bangladeshi freelancers say their income fell by up to 80%, and web development needs far fewer people than before.
    Source: LightCastle, August 2026; Daily Star, 20 September 2026.
    Confidence: Low. The freelancer reports are personal accounts, not a survey.
  • Claim: After H-1B visa limits, large tech companies increased hiring in India. The author's view is that this work goes where companies have their own offices, not to single remote workers, so Bangladesh does not benefit automatically.
    Source: Rest of World, date not given.
    Confidence: Low. The India trend is reported; the link to Bangladesh is the author's inference.
  • Claim: As of September 2026, a PayPal account cannot be opened with a Bangladeshi address. Bangladesh Bank issued a framework for cross-border digital payments on 29 July 2026, but gave no start date.
    Source: Daily Star, date not given; nsave, date not given.
    Confidence: Medium. News and web sources; the situation may change.
  • Claim: A direct remote job is only one of four routes to the global market, and the narrowest for freshers. The others are export-oriented firms in Bangladesh (the most realistic first step), working abroad (language is the main barrier), and higher study abroad (60% of graduates in the ADB survey wanted this).
    Source: Research note on career paths (author's recommendation), 17 November 2026; ADB, survey 2018, published 2019.
    Confidence: Low. Author's recommendation; only the 60% figure comes from a survey.

What Bangladeshi software firms do

Most of the industry sells services to foreign clients, mainly in the US. One note sorts 50 well-known firms into six groups; the author chose the firms, and only some of the descriptions were checked against sources.

  • Claim: The US is the largest single market, bringing about 27% of IT export earnings, according to a 2025 report.
    Source: Banglanews24, 2025 report.
    Confidence: Low. Taken from a search summary; the original page could not be opened to check.
  • Claim: BASIS lists the top 10 export destinations as the US, Japan, the UK, South Korea, Norway, Denmark, the Netherlands, Hong Kong, Singapore and Canada. About 500 member companies export, and about 300 of them export regularly.
    Source: DigiBanglaTech, 27 January 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. Industry association figures, reported by a news site.
  • Claim: In FY2025–26, computer services exports were USD 684 million (+8.7%). Of this, 88% (USD 605 million) was IT-enabled services such as BPO and data processing; pure software exports were only USD 48 million, and computer consultancy fell 28.7%.
    Source: EPB data, via Daily Star, 25 October 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. Official export data, but BASIS has long said the official figures are lower than real earnings.
  • Claim: The CEO of Brain Station 23 says classic staff augmentation is shrinking: a foreign firm that used to take five engineers now takes three. The KOW Company's staff fell from over 600 to under 400, and it now hires only people who can work with AI.
    Source: Daily Star, 25 October 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. Statements by company leaders in one news report.
  • Claim: The largest group of firms builds and maintains web, mobile and enterprise software for foreign clients, does QA, and supplies engineers to client teams. Examples are Brain Station 23 (800+ staff), BJIT (about 80% Japan), Enosis Solutions (about 70% US), Cefalo (Norway) and SELISE (Switzerland and Europe).
    Source: Research note on Bangladeshi software firms (no link given), 17 November 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. The author checked these firms against sources, but the note gives no links.
  • Claim: Two groups give the most stable hiring. Development centres of foreign product firms (such as Therap, Optimizely and Samsung R&D Institute Bangladesh) hire through problem-solving and DSA tests and pay more; firms that build software for local banks and government (such as LEADS, Tiger IT and BRAC IT Services) need domain knowledge and hire many people, though they export little.
    Source: Research note on Bangladeshi software firms (no link given), 17 November 2026.
    Confidence: Low. Author's judgement; most of these company descriptions were not verified (Samsung R&D and Tiger IT were).
  • Claim: Local digital platforms (bKash, Nagad, Pathao, ShopUp, Chaldal, 10 Minute School) run systems with many users and need backend, mobile, data and security skills. A smaller group sells its own products worldwide, and Bangladesh has a notable place in WordPress plugins (weDevs, WPDeveloper, Ollyo).
    Source: Research note on Bangladeshi software firms (no link given), 17 November 2026.
    Confidence: Low. Mostly the author's earlier knowledge, not verified (Ollyo was checked).
  • Claim: One blog puts junior pay at BDT 20,000–45,000 a month and senior pay at BDT 100,000–220,000. It says remote engineers at foreign companies average about USD 41,000 a year, 2–4 times local pay.
    Source: DEV Community, January 2026.
    Confidence: Low. A single blog; not verified.

Global skill shortages

Employers still report hiring difficulty, but the shortage is in specific skills and in experienced people. Working abroad is possible, but language is a major barrier.

  • Claim: In a 2026 survey of more than 39,000 employers in 41 countries, 72% had trouble filling roles, down from 77% in 2023. For the first time, AI skills were the hardest to find; traditional IT and data skills fell to seventh place.
    Source: ManpowerGroup, 2026.
    Confidence: High. A large survey across many countries.
  • Claim: Foundry/CIO's State of the CIO 2026 lists the hardest IT roles to fill as AI/ML and cybersecurity (joint top), then data science/analytics, automation, risk management, software engineering, DevOps/DevSecOps, enterprise architecture, cloud integration, cloud architecture and UX. Application development has dropped off the list completely, and "hybrid" roles that need both AI and business knowledge stay open for 6–9 months.
    Source: CIO, 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. One survey; the notes do not give its sample size.
  • Claim: ISC2 did not publish a workforce-gap number this year, because respondents said skills are a bigger problem than headcount. 59% of teams report critical skills gaps (44% the year before), and the most needed skills are AI (41%) and cloud security (36%).
    Source: ISC2 Cybersecurity Workforce Study, 2025.
    Confidence: High. The study's own findings. Note that the often-quoted "4.8 million gap" is a 2024 figure.
  • Claim: In Germany, vacant IT posts fell from 149,000 in 2023 to 79,000 in 2026, almost halved in three years. Only 6% of companies with vacancies hire from abroad, and 47% say the German language is a barrier.
    Source: Bitkom, 3 September 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. A single source, Germany only.
  • Claim: Japan's METI estimated in 2019 that Japan could be short of up to 790,000 IT workers by 2030. Through the B-JET programme, 280 Bangladeshis were trained up to 2020 and 186 of them (about 70%) found jobs in Japan; the University of Miyazaki and North South University now run the programme.
    Source: JICA, date not given.
    Confidence: Medium. The estimate is from 2019, and the B-JET figures stop at 2020.
  • Claim: The shortage is real for AI/ML engineering, security, data, cloud/DevOps, architecture, and experienced people who understand both technology and business. For general application and web developers and for fresh graduates there is no shortage, but rather a surplus.
    Source: Research notes (author's synthesis of the surveys above), 17 November 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. Author's synthesis, not a published finding, but based on several surveys that agree.
  • Claim: The roles in short supply need experience, but the first step to gaining experience, the junior job, is shrinking. The notes conclude that the syllabus should make graduates more useful than a typical junior from the first day.
    Source: Research note on three claims about CSE (author's recommendation), 17 November 2026.
    Confidence: Low. Author's recommendation.

Semiconductor and hardware sector

IT hiring surveys rarely count hardware jobs, and the hardware industry in Bangladesh is small. But the semiconductor sector now has active government policy and investment.

  • Claim: Deloitte forecasts that more than one million additional skilled semiconductor workers will be needed worldwide by 2030. It mentions a particular shortage in VLSI design.
    Source: Deloitte, date not given.
    Confidence: Medium. A forecast.
  • Claim: The SIA estimates a shortage of 67,000 semiconductor workers in the US by 2030. This demand is closer to EEE and hardware than to CSE.
    Source: SIA estimate, from a secondary source (no link given), date not given.
    Confidence: Low. Taken from a secondary source that the notes do not name.
  • Claim: Bangladesh's semiconductor sector has about 1,200 engineers and earns USD 12–15 million a year. The target is 10,000 skilled workers and USD 1 billion by 2030, and raw materials for chip design, test and packaging have a tax exemption until June 2031.
    Source: Daily Star, 24 July 2026.
    Confidence: Low. The figures come from industry bodies, which have an interest in promoting the sector.
  • Claim: Most graduates need about one year of specialised VLSI training before they are ready for industry. There are also too few experienced engineers to lead complex projects.
    Source: TBS, 23 June 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. One news report.
  • Claim: Bangladeshi chip firms today mainly do physical, analog, layout and RF design. Test and packaging are at an early stage; the national roadmap aims for three test facilities and two packaging industries.
    Source: Research note on career paths (no link given for this point), 17 November 2026.
    Confidence: Low. The notes give no source link for this point.
  • Claim: Reaching USD 1 billion from today's earnings would mean roughly 70–80 times growth in four years, which the author finds unrealistic. Jobs will grow, but in the thousands, not the tens of thousands; if universities can fill the "one year of training" gap, that is the real opportunity.
    Source: Research note on career paths (author's assessment), 17 November 2026.
    Confidence: Low. Author's assessment, not a published finding.
  • Claim: A hardware track needs microcontroller and FPGA boards, measuring instruments, open-source EDA tools at first, part-time teachers from industry, and internship agreements with design firms. Teachers are the biggest barrier, and the track should take no more than 20–30 students, matching the size of the market.
    Source: Research note on career paths (author's recommendation), 17 November 2026.
    Confidence: Low. Author's recommendation.

Evidence by track

Each track below lists evidence on jobs, demand and where graduates are hired. Some items repeat evidence from the sections above.

SWE — Software Engineering & Cloud

  • Claim: Software engineering, DevOps/DevSecOps and cloud roles remain on the CIO 2026 list of hardest IT roles to fill, but application development has dropped off. In the US, 71% of the recent growth in software postings is in senior roles.
    Source: CIO, 2026; Indeed Hiring Lab, 8 July 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. Mainly US data; no Bangladesh figures.
  • Claim: In Bangladesh, the largest group of firms builds software and supplies engineers for foreign clients, but Brain Station 23 says this staff augmentation is shrinking. Development centres of foreign product firms are a steadier employer; they hire through problem-solving and DSA tests and pay more.
    Source: Daily Star, 25 October 2026; research note on Bangladeshi software firms (no link given), 17 November 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. The staff-augmentation trend comes from one news report; the note on development centres is the author's judgement and mostly not verified.
  • Claim: QA and test automation is an entry path for mid-level students. 6% of German companies with vacancies are looking for QA staff, and US projections show +15% for developers, QA analysts and testers; manual testing is shrinking fast.
    Source: Bitkom, 3 September 2026; BLS, projection period 2024–34.
    Confidence: Low. The demand signal is moderate. The idea that more AI-written code means more testing work is the author's reasoning, not a measurement.
  • Claim: Banks, mobile financial services, telecoms, factories and government offices need people to run and change their systems, implement ERP and work as business analysts. In the 2018 ADB survey, finance and insurance were among the large employers of CSE graduates, and business/IT automation is fourth on the CIO 2026 list.
    Source: ADB, survey 2018, published 2019; CIO, 2026.
    Confidence: Low. No separate statistics were found; calling this the most stable path inside Bangladesh is the author's judgement.
  • Claim: Open posts for forward deployed engineers grew 4.6 times in one year. Hybrid roles that need both technology and business knowledge stay open for 6–9 months; the notes use both points as evidence for solutions engineering.
    Source: Lenny's Newsletter, 10 November 2026; Dice, date not given; CIO, 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. Mainly US data. The author notes that AI is taking over first-level support work.

AID — AI & Data

  • Claim: LinkedIn's 2026 list names AI engineer as the fastest-growing job in the US. ManpowerGroup's 2026 survey finds that AI skills are now the hardest skills to find worldwide.
    Source: LinkedIn list via Dice, date not given; ManpowerGroup, 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. The LinkedIn list is reported second-hand; ManpowerGroup is a large survey.
  • Claim: Of the recent growth in US software job postings, 37% is in roles with "AI" in the title.
    Source: Indeed Hiring Lab, 8 July 2026.
    Confidence: High. Direct data from the job site, but US only.
  • Claim: On Upwork, AI-related work pays on average more than 40% more per hour than other work. AI integration and chatbot development are among the fastest-growing coding sub-skills.
    Source: Upwork data, via Daily Star, 20 September 2026; LightCastle, August 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. Platform data reported second-hand.
  • Claim: Data science/analytics is third on the CIO 2026 list of hardest IT roles to fill, and the WEF lists big data specialists among the fastest-growing jobs. The analyst side of data work also suits mid-level students.
    Source: CIO, 2026; WEF Future of Jobs 2025, 2025.
    Confidence: Medium. Two surveys agree. The point about mid-level students, and that AI is taking routine reporting, is the author's judgement.
  • Claim: Work on preparing and checking data for AI models, and grading model answers, is growing: LinkedIn's 2026 list places data annotator near the top, and AI data annotation is growing fast on Upwork. LightCastle recommends this work for Bangladesh's surplus CSE graduates; it is remote and open to freshers.
    Source: LinkedIn 2026 list and Upwork data (no link given for this point); LightCastle, August 2026.
    Confidence: Low. No figures given. The author warns that pay for simple annotation is falling and sees this as a bridge, not a lasting career.

SEC — Cybersecurity

  • Claim: US projections for 2024–34 show information security analyst jobs growing 29%, against 15% for software developers, QA analysts and testers.
    Source: BLS, projection period 2024–34.
    Confidence: Medium. Official US projection; forecasts made during fast AI change are uncertain.
  • Claim: In the ISC2 2025 study, 59% of teams report critical skills gaps, up from 44% the year before. Cybersecurity shares the top place with AI/ML on the CIO 2026 list of hardest IT roles to fill.
    Source: ISC2, 2025; CIO, 2026.
    Confidence: High. Two separate surveys agree.
  • Claim: A report on students at DU, BUET and NSU says there is demand for cybersecurity in Bangladesh, but no teachers to teach it.
    Source: Daily Star, 2 July 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. One news report.
  • Claim: SOC analyst and governance and compliance (GRC) roles suit mid-level students, and banks and telecoms in Bangladesh have demand for them. The KOW Company, an image-editing firm, is also moving into AI work and cybersecurity.
    Source: Research notes on career paths and on Bangladeshi software firms (no link given), 17 November 2026.
    Confidence: Low. Author's judgement; no Bangladesh hiring statistics.

INF — Systems, Networks & Infrastructure

  • Claim: In Germany, the profile most wanted by companies is IT administration and system operation (26% of companies). It is ahead of software development (17%) and cloud and infrastructure (14%).
    Source: Bitkom, 3 September 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. A single source, Germany only.
  • Claim: DevOps/DevSecOps, cloud integration and cloud architecture are on the CIO 2026 list of hardest IT roles to fill, and DevOps and cloud integration are moving up the list.
    Source: CIO, 2026.
    Confidence: Medium. One survey; the notes do not give its sample size.
  • Claim: In the Uptime Institute 2026 survey, 53% of data centre operators had trouble finding qualified staff, up from 46% the year before. US projections show data centre technician jobs growing 14% in 2024–34.
    Source: Introl, date not given.
    Confidence: Medium. Both figures come from a secondary source, not the original reports.
  • Claim: In Bangladesh, banks, telecoms and ISPs employ data centre and network staff; abroad, the Middle East and Southeast Asia do. The notes call cloud, DevOps and IT operations one of the best paths for mid-level students, and data centre work the most reliable hardware-related path, since the work is physical and AI is driving more data-centre building.
    Source: Research note on career paths (author's judgement), 17 November 2026.
    Confidence: Low. Author's judgement; no Bangladesh hiring statistics.

EMB — Embedded Systems & IoT

  • Claim: Evidence for embedded and firmware demand is weak. A recruiting firm's blog says firmware posts take about 102 days to fill, against 60 days for general engineering posts; the notes also name Walton, Samsung R&D Institute Bangladesh and some local firms as employers in Bangladesh.
    Source: Paraform, date not given.
    Confidence: Low. One recruiter's blog; no neutral survey was found.
  • Claim: Among the 50 software firms in the notes, the only notable hardware-related employers are Samsung R&D Institute Bangladesh, BJIT's embedded unit and ReliSource.
    Source: Research note on Bangladeshi software firms (no link given), 17 November 2026.
    Confidence: Low. The author's own list of firms, not a full survey of the sector.
  • Claim: Chip design verification and RTL design is the most natural route from CSE into hardware, because verification is much like software work: writing tests, measuring coverage, debugging. Jobs are in Bangladeshi design firms, India, Malaysia and Vietnam, and in the US and Europe after higher study.
    Source: Research note on career paths (author's judgement), 17 November 2026; Deloitte, date not given.
    Confidence: Low. Author's judgement; the global demand figure from Deloitte is a forecast.
  • Claim: Garment, pharmaceutical and food factories are adding automation, which creates robotics and PLC work in Bangladesh and the Middle East. FPGA work also exists, but the market is small.
    Source: Research note on career paths (author's observation), 17 November 2026.
    Confidence: Low. A general observation; no Bangladesh-specific statistics were found.
  • Claim: Electronics product and PCB jobs are mainly in mobile phone and home appliance assembly plants. These plants mostly assemble, so design work is limited, and the path is more natural for EEE graduates.
    Source: Research note on career paths (author's judgement), 17 November 2026.
    Confidence: Low. Author's judgement; no statistics given.

Limits of the evidence

  • Almost all strong statistics on hiring and AI come from the US. Most other demand figures come from Germany and Japan. The notes found no BASIS or government hiring statistics, and no hiring data by job type for Bangladesh.
  • Many Bangladesh figures (number of graduates, number of freelancers, income) differ between sources and are estimates.
  • Some figures come from secondary analyses, not the original reports. This page marks them where the notes do.
  • No published study compares a Bangladeshi CSE syllabus with ACM/IEEE CS2023 course by course. The notes checked only a few syllabi: ULAB and UIU include software testing and cloud computing, but cloud is usually an elective, and no DevOps or CI/CD course was found.
  • For embedded systems, robotics, enterprise systems and "CSE plus another field", there are no reliable statistics. The assessments there are the author's judgement.
  • Bangladesh semiconductor figures come from industry bodies that have an interest in promoting the sector.
  • The list of 50 software firms is the author's choice. Only 11 firms were checked against sources; the rest come from the author's earlier knowledge and may be out of date.
  • The advice in the notes (what to add to or cut from the syllabus, the content of each career path, who each path suits, and the five-year AI risk) is the author's own judgement. None of it has been tested in controlled research.
  • Five-year forecasts are uncertain. If AI progress speeds up or slows down, the risk ratings will change.
  • The notes changed one view: one note suggests cutting 8086 assembly and peripheral interfacing, but the career note says these topics are central for the embedded track, taught with modern microcontrollers instead of the 8086.
