2. How each course file is laid out

Every course file has the same nine sections.

  1. Course facts. Copied from the outline without change: header table, CLO table and minimum competencies.
  2. Class pattern. The assumption about class length, stated for confirmation.
  3. Theory lesson plan. 28 classes, two per week. Each class has:
    • CLO and PO;
    • contents with a timed breakdown of 90 minutes;
    • TLA (teaching-learning activities);
    • materials;
    • an in-class formative check;
    • homework with its AI-use level.
  4. Lab lesson plan. 14 sessions of 120 minutes. Each session has:
    • aims;
    • timed activities;
    • the deliverable;
    • the AI-use level;
    • how the work is checked.
  5. Assignments. Full briefs for the theory assignment and the lab project.
  6. Assessment strategy. This section has:
    • a calendar;
    • examination blueprints;
    • sample questions with answers or marking notes;
    • practical-test tasks;
    • the project and viva rubrics;
    • the CLO-to-assessment-to-PO mapping;
    • CLO attainment.
  7. Support for weaker students.
  8. Course teacher responsibilities and minimum qualification.
  9. Issues for the committee.

Class headings read #### Class N — Week W: title. Lab headings read #### Session N — Week W: title. Classes are numbered 1 to 28 and sessions 1 to 14.

4. AI-use rules in practice

4.1 The levels in this track

The framework (section 6) defines the levels. The outline header row of each course fixes them. They are copied here so that every brief and every lab can point to one table.

CourseExams and quizzesAssignmentsLab workLab projectLab tests and viva
CSE-3671/3672022, except weeks 1–3: Level 0 (code reading unaided)20
CSE-4771/4772022, except weeks 1–2: Level 120
CSE-4781/4782022, except weeks 2–4: Level 1 (test design by hand) and week 12: Level 3 (an AI feature is the system under test)20
CSE-4881/488202220. Every quiz, practical test and viva is on campus in weeks 7–14.

What the levels mean for a student, in plain words:

  • Level 0. No AI tool of any kind. This includes chat assistants, code completion in the editor, and AI search summaries. Lab machines in examination mode enforce this.
  • Level 1. AI may explain a concept or an error message. It may not write any part of the answer: no code, no test, no table, no text that is submitted.
  • Level 2. AI may write code and text with you, as a pair programmer. You must:
    • keep the usage log (template 6.2);
    • put the disclosure line on the submission;
    • test what the AI wrote;
    • be able to explain every line in the viva.
  • Level 3. The AI system is what you are building or testing. Levels 2 and 3 have the same rules about logging and checking.

4.2 How the rules are enforced

  1. State it. Every brief, lab sheet and test paper states its level at the top.
  2. Enforce Level 0 by setting, not by suspicion. Use the examination login (assumption A11), seat students with gaps, keep phones in bags, and have two invigilators in practical tests. Do not use "AI-detector" tools on text or code. They are not reliable and must not be used as evidence.
  3. Check Level 2 by explanation. The usage log and the viva are the checks. In every lab check-off, ask one "why is this here?" question about the student's own work.
  4. If the rules seem broken. First, ask the student to explain the work, orally, at Level 0. If they cannot, and the work is a Level 0 item or the use was not disclosed, report it under the university's academic-integrity procedure. Do not set a penalty of your own. The framework's rule stands: a student who cannot explain their own submitted work does not pass the lab.
  5. Missing log. At Level 2, a missing or empty log costs the log's marks in the rubric. It is not treated as misconduct unless the work is also unexplained.

5. Shared marking schemes

5.1 Weekly lab work and record (30 marks)

Lab record10The record is the student's repository: a lab-record.md file with one short entry per session, plus the AI-usage log. It is checked twice, once before each practical test, 5 marks each time.

ScoreMeaning

How check-off works in the room: the instructor walks the room from the middle of the session. In the last 25 minutes, the instructor checks each student who has not yet been seen, for about one minute each: run it, ask one question. In weeks where the work is in pairs or teams, each student answers for their own part.

A student absent with a documented reason may complete the session from the catch-up branch or sheet within one week, for at most 1 mark.

Lab record rubric (5 marks at each check).

CriterionMarks

Each entry says what was done, gives evidence (command output, link to the pull request, a measurement) and states one thing learned2

5.2 Supervised practical tests (2 × 15 marks)

  • When: the weeks the outline gives (CSE-3672: weeks 7 and 13; CSE-4772: 7 and 13; CSE-4782: 7 and 13; CSE-4882: 8 and 13).
  • Level 0, examination login, on lab machines. Allowed: offline official documentation, and a one-page course reference sheet issued by the instructor. Not allowed: AI tools, the open internet, messaging, the student's own repositories and notes.
  • Timing: 10 minutes for set-up and reading, 100 minutes of work, 10 minutes for submission. The submission is a commit to a local repository, collected by the instructor, together with an answer sheet.
  • Two parallel versions. Lab groups that sit on different days get different but equal versions, so that the content does not leak. The lab instructor writes both versions and pilots them before the test week (section 8).
  • Marking: by running the submission and by reading the answer sheet. Each course file gives the task-level marking scheme. Correct reasoning written on the answer sheet earns partial marks even when the code does not run. This matters for weaker students.
  • Absence: one make-up sitting, in the examination period, for absence with a documented reason, under the department's rule.

5.3 Individual viva (15 marks)

Protocol.

  • The viva is at Level 0, on campus, at a lab machine with the student's own repository open.
  • It takes about 8 minutes per student, with two examiners working in parallel: the lab instructor and the theory teacher, or a second teacher.
  • It has four parts:
    1. The student shows their own part of the work (1 minute).
    2. The examiner asks why chosen pieces are where they are (2 minutes).
    3. A short live task, set by the course file (4 minutes).
    4. One concept question tied to a CLO (1 minute).
  • The examiner records a score and a short note for each criterion on the viva record sheet (template 6.8).

Generic rubric. Each course file adapts the live task and the question bank. The criteria and marks stay the same.

CriterionMarks0–1MiddleTop
A. Explains own work5Cannot say what their own code or document does or why it is thereExplains most of it with prompting; some gapsExplains clearly without prompting, including the choices made and the options rejected
B. Reasons about change4Cannot say what a stated change would affectNames the direct effectsNames direct and indirect effects and how to check them (tests, metrics, logs)
C. Live task at Level 04Cannot startCompletes it with hintsCompletes it unaided and explains each step
D. Course concepts applied2Wrong or missing termsCorrect terms, loosely appliedCorrect terms tied to their own work

The "cannot explain" rule (framework section 7).

  • A score of 0 or 1 on criterion A, confirmed by the second examiner, records that the student has not explained their own work.
  • The lab is then not passed, whatever the other marks.
  • The record sheet says which parts could not be explained.
  • Proposal for the committee: whether a second viva is offered before results are published (see issue T4).

5.4 Lab projects (25 marks)

Each course file gives the project criteria. They all use this scale:

LevelMeaningShare of the criterion's marks
0 MissingNot present, or does not work0
1 BelowPresent but incomplete or incorrect in important waysabout one third
2 AcceptableMeets the briefabout two thirds
3 StrongMeets the brief, with evidence and reasoning beyond itfull

Team and pair projects.

  • Marks are split into a team part and an individual part. Each course file gives the split.
  • The team part is multiplied by a contribution factor, normally 1.0. It is reduced (to 0.75, 0.5 or 0) only on evidence: the repository history, the team board, the peer assessment and the check-offs.
  • The individual part marks the student's own slice, feature or document.
  • Peer assessment uses a one-line-per-member form at the end of the project. The instructor reads it; it does not change marks by itself.

5.5 Writing rubric (ADRs, reviews, defect reports, postmortems)

The track's written work is in English, because that is the language of work with foreign clients. The rubric marks whether the writing does its job, not whether the grammar is perfect. It is used inside project and weekly marks, not as a separate mark.

CriterionWeakAcceptableStrong
Uses the templateParts missingAll parts presentAll parts present and in proportion
SpecificGeneral statements ("the code is bad")Names files, requests, numbersNames files, requests and numbers, and states the effect on users
EvidenceNoneSome (logs, measurements, screenshots)Evidence for every claim
UsableA classmate must ask questions to act on itA classmate can act on itA classmate can act on it quickly, and knows what is still unknown

Grammar errors are not penalised unless they change or hide the meaning. Discussion in class may be in Bangla. Submitted text is in English.

5.6 Quizzes (continuous assessment, 10 marks)

  • 15 minutes, at the start of a class, on paper, at Level 0, closed book (assumption A8).
  • Each quiz is marked out of 10. The course total is scaled to 10 marks, by the outline's rule:
    • CSE-3671: four quizzes, best three counted;
    • CSE-4771, CSE-4781 and CSE-4881: three quizzes.
  • Questions are short application items: read code, derive cases, read a manifest. At most 2 of the 10 marks are recall.
  • Scripts are returned in the next class with a model answer. Where a best-of rule applies, there is no make-up quiz. Otherwise one make-up is given for documented absence, under the department's rule.

5.7 Written examinations (80 marks)

Rules from framework section 7, applied to every paper:

  • At least half of the written marks (40 of 80) test application and analysis. In these plans that means Bloom levels Apply, Analyse, Evaluate and Create: reading and tracing code, finding defects, choosing between designs, working a case.
  • At most a fifth (16 of 80) test recall (Bloom: Remember).
  • Each course file gives a blueprint for the mid-term (30, weeks 1–7) and the final (50, whole course). The blueprint lists each question with marks, CLO and Bloom level, and checks both rules.

Moderation checklist (for the moderator, before the paper is printed).

  1. Does every question carry a CLO tag and a Bloom tag?
  2. Do the Bloom totals meet the rules: recall at most 20%, Apply and above at least 50%?
  3. Is there a marking scheme with the points that earn marks?
  4. Does the paper fit the time? Test: the setter writes the model answer in at most half the time allowed.
  5. Is the English plain? Short sentences, and no unfamiliar words except course terms.
  6. Is any question copied from a previous paper or from a sample in this file? Samples are for practice and must not be reused unchanged.

7. CLO attainment for outcome-based education

The same method is used in all four courses. Thresholds are proposals (assumption A12).

  1. Tag every assessed item with one CLO. Items are exam questions, quizzes, assignment criteria, practical-test tasks, project criteria and viva parts. If an item serves two CLOs, split its marks between them in the blueprint.
  2. Theory and lab are computed separately. They are separate course codes with separate grades. The course report shows both side by side.
  3. Student score per CLO. Score = marks obtained on the items tagged with that CLO ÷ marks available on those items × 100. Items not attempted count as zero. A student who missed an item with a documented reason and took a make-up uses the make-up marks.
  4. Student attainment. A student attains a CLO when the score is at least 50%.
  5. Class attainment. The class attains a CLO when at least 60% of the students who completed the course attain it.
  6. PO attainment. Each CLO's attainment is reported against its mapped POs from the outline. The department's OBE process combines courses into programme-level PO attainment. These files do not set that method.
  7. Recording.
    • The theory teacher keeps the attainment sheet (template 6.10) for theory.
    • The lab instructor keeps one for the lab.
    • Both sheets go into the course file within two weeks of results.
  8. Closing the loop.
    • For any CLO below target, the course report states the likely cause and one change for the next offering. Examples: more practice in the lab, a changed quiz, a re-ordered week.
    • The next teacher reads that report before the semester starts.

Worked example. In CSE-3671 theory, suppose 34 marks of the 100 are tagged to CLO 2. A student scores 19 of them: 19 ÷ 34 = 56%, so the student attains CLO 2. If 71 of 110 students attain it, 71 ÷ 110 = 65%. That is above the 60% target, so the class attains CLO 2.

8. Duties shared by every SWE course teacher

Each course file lists the duties specific to that course. These apply to all four.

Theory teacher

  • Before the semester:
    • read the previous offering's course report and attainment sheets;
    • check the library holds the main text;
    • publish this lesson plan, the assessment calendar and the AI-use table to students in week 1.
  • Every week:
    • teach the two classes;
    • run the formative checks and note common errors;
    • meet the lab instructor for 15 minutes to align theory and lab.
  • Assessment and moderation:
    • set papers to the blueprint;
    • send them to a moderator two weeks before the examination;
    • double-mark a 10% sample, including every script within 3 marks of a grade boundary;
    • return quizzes within a week.
  • AI-use rules: state the level on every item, and apply section 4.2.
  • Evidence: keep the course file (section 8.1).
  • Coordination: with the other track courses and the capstone, as each course file states.

Lab instructor

  • Before the semester:
    • build the lab environment on a lab machine;
    • prepare the offline mirrors;
    • write and pilot both practical tests in two versions;
    • prepare the catch-up branch or sheet for each session.
  • Every week:
    • publish the session sheet one week ahead;
    • run the session;
    • do the check-offs and record them within two days.
  • Assessment:
    • mark the practical tests within a week;
    • run the viva with a second examiner;
    • keep the viva record sheets.
  • AI-use rules:
    • make sure the examination login is in place before each practical test and the viva;
    • read a sample of usage logs each fortnight.

10. Support for weaker students: measures used in all four courses

The intake is about 15% strong, 60% mid-level and 25% below average. Each course file adds its own measures. These apply everywhere:

  1. A checked floor. The minimum competencies are printed on the first lab sheet. Each is practised at least twice before a practical test tests it.
  2. Worked example first, then a faded example, then an independent task. Every theory class with a new technique follows this order.
  3. Plain English and a glossary. Each course keeps a one-page glossary of terms, with a short Bangla gloss where it helps. Discussion may be in Bangla; submitted work is in English.
  4. Catch-up material. A catch-up branch or sheet is published for every lab session, so that missing one week does not block the next.
  5. Early warning. After practical test 1 and the mid-term, the teacher lists students below 40% in either. Each is invited to a 30-minute clinic with a concrete plan of two or three tasks.
  6. Clinics. One optional clinic hour a week, at a time that suits students who travel. It can be online in semester 8.
  7. Partial marks for reasoning in practical tests and examinations (section 5.2).
  8. Pairing with care. Strong students are not paired with each other in weeks where pairs are used. Every pair member must answer for their own part at check-off.
  9. Stretch work for the strongest 15%. It comes from the outline's stretch goals. It is optional and does not add marks beyond the rubric's "strong" level.
