Rationale and what changed

This course replaces Field Work in the 2022 syllabus. Vacations are too short for an internship, the intake is too large for every student to be placed on site, and the campus is far from employers, so the course accepts 200 hours of supervised professional work in any of four modes. Most students will be in modes B, C and D; these are designed as full routes with the same marks, not as second-best substitutes for an on-site internship. A student who cannot move to the city for family or cost reasons loses nothing. What every mode has in common is work that someone outside the student's own team accepts or rejects, a supervisor, and evidence for every logged hour.

Minimum competency

  • Complete 200 verified hours, with every logbook entry linked to evidence.
  • Deliver at least one piece of work accepted by someone outside their own team: a supervisor, a product owner or a project maintainer.
  • Write a weekly status update in English that the supervisor accepts without rewriting.
  • Explain any logged task in the viva: what was asked, what was done, how it was checked.

Stretch goals

  • An offer of employment, a paid extension or a repeat client arising from the placement.
  • A contribution merged into a widely used open-source project, or leading a sprint in the virtual studio.

Rules for all modes:

  • An hour counts only with evidence and the supervisor's weekly confirmation; at most 8 hours a day and 40 a week. Up to 80 hours may be logged in the break before semester 8 if the placement was approved before the work began.
  • No hour or piece of work is counted twice: not with the capstone, not with another course.
  • The host's confidentiality and AI-use rules override the university's. Work under a confidentiality agreement is described at the level the host approves and may be shown on screen at the viva instead of being submitted.
  • A stipend is welcome and not required. The coordinator keeps a named contact for complaints; a host that gives no real work or mistreats students is removed from the register.
  • In Mode D, students follow each project's contribution and AI policies, the mentor reviews every pull request before it is sent during the first four weeks, and no more than five students work on one project in a semester unless its maintainers agree. Seventy-five students sending weak pull requests would damage the university's name with the projects.

Partner pipeline the department must build

Work starts a year before the first cohort reaches semester 8.

  1. People. A coordinator (a senior teacher with reduced teaching), an office assistant, and one liaison teacher per track.
  2. Partner register for A and B. Firms with a signed memorandum stating places per semester, mode and supervisor. At the largest intake about 20 to 27 firms offering three places each cover the internship share. Software firms serving clients in the United States and Europe come first, because that is the graduates' market.
  3. Alumni register. Alumni in firms at home and abroad, as referrers, Mode C mentors (one hour a week online) and mock interviewers.
  4. Backlog bank for C. Product owners with work for a team of five or six for 200 hours each: partner firms' internal tools, university offices, NGOs, local organisations, alumni start-ups. Prefer long-lived products that pass from cohort to cohort, so that students inherit a codebase as they would at work; about 10 to 15 such products, each hosting two teams on separate parts, serve the largest intake.
  5. Project list and mentors for D. An approved list reviewed every semester, and three or four teachers who have themselves had contributions merged.
  6. Documents. Memorandum and acceptance-letter templates, the logbook, the evaluation form, guidance on confidentiality agreements, and a conduct and complaints procedure.
  7. Pilot. Run Mode C and Mode D with volunteers one semester before the first full cohort.

Mode C needs 14–20 teams at an intake of 200 and 20–30 at 300. With groups staggered over the three practice days, it needs two 40-seat labs at 200 and two or three at 300. Because there are two intakes a year, this load recurs every semester.

A student with no placement

  • No student starts semester 8 without a mode. Anyone without a confirmed A or B placement at the week 13 allocation is placed in C or D by the coordinator, with their preference considered.
  • If a placement collapses during the semester (the host withdraws, the contract ends), verified hours are kept and the student moves to C or D within one week to complete the balance.
  • A student short of 200 hours in week 14 receives an Incomplete and finishes in a completion sprint (Mode C or D) in the following break; failing that, the course is repeated, and it runs every semester.
  • A student dismissed for misconduct, or found to have falsified hours or evidence, fails the course and is referred under the university's disciplinary rules.

Logbook

A shared spreadsheet or the department's form, one row per working day: date; hours; task; link to evidence (ticket, commit, pull request, document, tracker record); what was learned; what blocked progress. Each week ends with a five-line summary and next week's plan, confirmed by the supervisor (A, B1, B2), the studio lead (C) or the faculty mentor (B3, D). The faculty mentor or liaison checks it every two weeks.

Supervisor's evaluation form

One page, completed at the mid-point (formative, discussed with the student) and at the end (counted). Eight items rated 1 to 5: technical competence for the tasks given; quality and completeness of work; speed of learning and independence; reliability (attendance, deadlines, commitments); written communication; spoken communication and part taken in meetings; teamwork and response to review; professional conduct (honesty, confidentiality, following policy). Then: hours confirmed; main tasks; strengths; what to improve; "Would you take this student again?" (yes, with reservations, no); name, role, contact and signature. The total out of 40 is scaled to 25. In B3 and D the faculty mentor completes the form from client feedback or from the public record of reviews and accepted contributions.

Reflective report

Individual, 2,000–2,500 words in English: the organisation or project, its product and how it earns or is funded; my role and tasks, with evidence; one technical problem I solved and how; what I learned about professional practice, including one ethical or professional question I met; how my work was judged and what I would do differently; the skills I lacked, and my learning plan for the next twelve months with resources and checkpoints.

Viva

Twelve minutes, individual, Level 0, before two examiners (the faculty mentor and one other teacher; industry guests where available). The examiners choose two logbook entries and one work product; the student explains what was asked, what was done and how it was checked, then answers one question on the learning plan. A student who cannot explain their logged work fails the course, whatever the other marks.

Hurdles: 200 verified hours; the viva; and in Mode D the accepted-contribution minimum. Supervisors in different modes mark differently, so the coordinator compares the distribution of the supervisor's mark by mode each semester and moderates where one mode is out of line; the viva, marked by teachers for every student, is the common measure.

Notes for the coordinator

In the planned public view this section is shown to the committee only (specification 3.4).

  • The risk in modes A and B is a placement with no real work. The week 2–3 verification call and the mid-point form exist to find these early enough to move the student.
  • The risk in Mode C is that it becomes another class project. It stays professional only while an outside product owner accepts or rejects the work and an industry mentor sees it every week; a studio without both should not run.
  • The risk in Mode D is weeks passing with nothing merged, because review by maintainers is slow. Start in week 1, choose projects known to respond, and use the week 10 checkpoint.
  • Freelance work (B3) is where falsification is easiest. Verify the account live on screen, check that the account is the student's own, and question the delivered work in the viva.
  • Count the liaison, studio-lead and mentor roles, and the viva panels, as teaching load. They fall on the same teachers, in the same weeks, as the capstone defences.

About this group of courses: Professional Practice, Capstone and Industry Practice

These six courses, all in semesters 7 and 8, carry most of the programme's evidence for outcomes PO6 to PO12; the technical courses carry PO1 to PO5. Each PO claimed here is tied to a named piece of marked work that can be kept on file for an accreditation visit. The courses feed one another: CSE-4753 and CSE-4760 run beside Capstone Project I and supply its plan, cost model, proposal and presentations, and CSE-4805 runs beside Capstone Project II and Industry Practice and takes its cases from them. Everything is specified for an intake of five to eleven sections twice a year (taken here as 200 to 300 students), so the supervision, panel and placement arithmetic is shown.
