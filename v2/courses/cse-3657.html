Course description (Flow B, section 6)

  • Aims. Find a real problem worth solving, test it with customers, and design a business that can pay for itself in Bangladesh's conditions. These skills also make graduates better employees.
  • Prerequisites. CSE-2433 and CSE-2434; taken with CSE-3660.
  • Content (weeks 1–14).
    1. Four routes into business (section 3.1); the Bangladeshi funding reality; forming teams.
    2. Finding problems: observation and the "jobs to be done" idea; local sectors and global niches.
    3. Customer interviews I: designing them, sampling, avoiding leading questions, consent.
    4. Customer interviews II: making sense of the notes; deciding whether there is enough evidence of a problem.
    5. Value proposition; competitors and substitutes; AI as a feature and as a way to build.
    6. Minimum viable product and experiments: landing page, concierge, "Wizard of Oz"; measures.
    7. Evidence review; mid-term.
    8. Business models: subscription, licence, freemium, marketplace, fixed-price and retainer services, freelance agency.
    9. Unit economics: acquisition cost, lifetime value, churn, gross margin with cloud and AI cost per user.
    10. Selling at home and abroad: B2B sales, tenders, marketplaces and directories, payments.
    11. Legal forms and founders' agreements; IP and licences (described generally).
    12. Funding: bootstrapping, grants and competitions, incubators, angel and venture investment, dilution.
    13. Writing the business case; ethics of products (dark patterns, data use).
    14. Pitch day with outside judges; the venture-option decision.
  • Weekly rhythm. One hour of input, then a two-hour workshop on the team's own venture. Teams of 4–5 do at least two hours of fieldwork a week outside class.
  • Assessment. University rule: written mid-term and final 80, continuous assessment 20.
    • Exams: case work — analyse interview notes, find untested assumptions, compute unit economics, choose a model and a price, critique a pitch. Recall is no more than a fifth.
    • Continuous assessment: venture evidence file 10, pitch 5, quizzes 5. This departs from Draft 1's proposed 10/10 split (open question 9).
  • AI use. Level 0 for exams, quizzes and pitch questions. Level 2 with disclosure for venture work: drafting canvases, landing pages and analysis. Interviews must be real and on file for audit; inventing one is misconduct.
