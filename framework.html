1. Brief

Decisions: Decision 1, Decision 2, Decision 3, Decision 4, Decision 5, Decision 6, Decision 7, Decision 8, Decision 9, Decision 11

The brief given for this draft:

  • Produce a complete syllabus whose graduates are industry-ready, or at least able to become ready by self-study within a year.
  • Design for the actual intake: roughly 15% strong students, 60% mid-level, 25% below average.
  • Follow industry need. The reference market is the global one that Bangladeshi firms already serve (mainly the United States), not only the domestic market.
  • Eight semesters, two per year. Total 140–145 credits including general education (GED). Lower weekly contact hours are preferred; the campus is outside the city, so every scheduled hour costs students travel time.
  • At most five theory courses per semester. Theory and lab belong to the same course (a theory course with its paired lab).
  • GED courses (GE*/UR* codes) and non-credit MDP courses stay as they are.
  • Everyone needs current AI skills: AI-assisted development, agentic workflows and building AI features.
  • One or two technical-communication courses. Vendor certification is not a priority.
  • The existing syllabi of Bangladeshi universities are not the reference point.

3. Design principles

Decisions: Decision 2, Decision 3

  1. Fewer courses, taught to competence. Excluding GED and MDP, the 2022 syllabus has 73 course units; this draft has 56 for any one student. The freed time goes to practice.
  2. Fundamentals are kept; rote production is cut. Data structures, algorithms, operating systems, databases, networks, architecture and discrete mathematics stay as full courses. What is cut is repeated syntax teaching, legacy hardware detail, and science courses with no downstream use in the programme.
  3. Do it by hand once, then with tools. Each basic skill is first done unaided (to build the mental model) and afterwards with libraries, frameworks and AI assistants (to reach realistic scale). Repetition for speed is no longer a goal.
  4. Every student can explain their own work. AI makes take-home work unreliable as evidence, so each lab has supervised practical tests and a viva, and each studio an individual viva.
  5. A checked floor for the middle 60%. Each course lists minimum competencies that every passing student must demonstrate, and a programme-level practical assessment in semester 4 gates entry to the final-year work.
  6. Tracks with different entry barriers. Five tracks lead to different job families, including ones where mid-level students do well (infrastructure, security operations, testing).
  7. Global-market readiness. English technical writing, public evidence of work (repositories), remote-team practices and optional certification alignment are built into courses rather than added as extra courses.

Credit and contact-hour rules

  • Theory: 1 credit = 1 contact hour per week.
  • Paired lab: 1 credit = 2 contact hours per week; 1.5 credits = 3 hours. Three-hour labs are kept where set-up and debugging time matters most: Programming I and II, Data Structures and Digital Logic Design. The two lab-only communication courses, Developer Tools & Technical Communication Lab and Technical & Professional Communication, are also 1.5 credits for 3 hours.
  • Studio (lab-only project course): credit is earned by scheduled studio hours plus logged project hours, 25 hours per credit in total. Software Engineering Studio: 3 credits, 4 scheduled hours. AI Application & Agent Engineering Studio: 3 credits, 3 scheduled hours.
  • Capstone: 3 + 5 credits with 1 and 2 scheduled supervision hours.
  • Industry Practice: 4 credits for at least 200 logged hours; no scheduled class hours.
  • Core Competency Assessment: 1 credit, pass/fail, earned by passing the practical examination (section 8); preparation and the optional clinics make up the notional hours.
  • Semester length: outlines assume 14 teaching weeks.

The studio, capstone and practice rules depart from the "1 lab credit = 2 contact hours" rule. This is deliberate: project work does not need a scheduled room for every hour, and scheduled hours are what cost students travel. The basis is the accreditation body's own definition of a laboratory credit: 60 notional hours of student work, of which at least 25 are spent in laboratory, studio, project or dissertation work. Studios and the capstone meet the 25-hour minimum with scheduled plus logged supervised hours. Industry Practice asks for 200 logged hours for 4 credits (50 per credit), above the minimum and within the 60-hour notional workload.

6. AI in the curriculum

Related: AI-use levels

Decisions: Decision 15

AI is treated three ways: as a tool whose use is staged, as a subject everyone studies, and as a specialisation.

Staged use. Every outline states the permitted level for each assessed component.

LevelMeaningWhere
0No AI assistanceProgramming I; all quizzes, written exams, supervised practical tests and vivas in every course
1AI as tutor: may explain concepts and errors, may not write the solutionSemester 1 assignments outside Programming I; weekly lab work and assignments in semesters 2–5; other lab projects in semesters 2–3
2AI as pair programmer, with disclosure: usage is logged, output is tested, the student defends every lineThe Object-Oriented Programming & Design team project (semester 3); lab projects and studios from semester 4; weekly lab work and assignments in semesters 6–8
3AI is the material: building, evaluating and operating AI systemsAI studio, AI & Data track, parts of other tracks

A course may set a stricter level than this table for named weeks, for example the opening "by hand" weeks of a track course; it may not set a looser one.

For everyone. Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning (S5, 3+1) gives the foundations. AI Application & Agent Engineering Studio (S6, 3 credits) has every student ship a deployed product with an AI feature, built with an AI-assisted workflow: LLM APIs, retrieval, tool use, agents, evaluation, cost and safety.

For specialists. The AI & Data track adds four courses. It needs stronger mathematics; the core AI courses do not.

7. Assessment rules

Related: Assessment schemes

Decisions: Decision 14, Decision 31

Theory courses. The university rule stands: written mid-term and final examinations carry 80 marks, continuous assessment 20. What changes is the questions:

  • At least half of the written marks test application and analysis: reading and tracing code, finding and fixing defects, choosing between designs, working a case.
  • At most a fifth of the written marks test recall.
  • Continuous assessment (20): short in-class quizzes at Level 0 (10) and assignments or a mini-project (10).

Paired labs (100 marks).

ComponentMarks
Weekly lab work and lab record30
Two supervised practical tests, on lab machines or at the bench for hardware labs, Level 030
Lab project25
Individual viva on the student's own work15

A student who cannot explain their own submitted work in the viva does not pass the lab, whatever the project mark.

Studios (100 marks). Team product against milestones 40; individual contribution evidenced by commits and reviews 20; individual viva 20; documentation and demonstration 20.

8. Core Competency Assessment (CSE-2440, semester 4)

Related: Core Competency Assessment

Decisions: Decision 20

A one-credit, pass/fail practical examination held on lab machines at Level 0. It checks that the student can, unaided: write and debug a small program from a specification; choose and use an appropriate data structure; write SQL queries against a given schema; work in a Linux shell with Git; and read and explain unfamiliar code. It is offered twice each semester: sitting 1 in week 10 of semester 4, once Database Systems has covered the SQL the assessment needs, and sitting 2 in week 14, with remedial clinics in weeks 11–13. Stations already passed are kept. A pass is required before registering for the semester-7 track courses (Track Courses II and III) and Capstone Project I.

9. Tracks

Related: Tracks

Decisions: Decision 19

Each student takes one track of four courses (3-credit theory with 1-credit lab each): one in semester 6, two in semester 7 and one in semester 8. The capstone is done in the track's area. Tracks are chosen at the end of semester 5.

TrackLeads toEntry profile
SWE — Software Engineering & CloudProduct and service engineering, DevOpsAny; the largest track
AID — AI & DataAI application engineer, data engineer, analystStrong in mathematics and programming
SEC — CybersecuritySOC analyst, application security, governance and complianceMid-level to strong
INF — Systems, Networks & InfrastructureIT operations, cloud infrastructure, data centre, network engineerWell suited to mid-level students
EMB — Embedded Systems & IoTFirmware, IoT, entry to chip design verificationInterest in hardware; small cohort

Courses are listed in 01-semester-plan.md.

Overlap. Track Course I (semester 6) is each track's foundation and cannot be replaced. A student may replace one of Track Courses II–IV with a course from another track offered in the same semester, with the adviser's approval. A course that is a prerequisite of the student's own semester-8 track course cannot be swapped; in practice an AID student may swap only CSE-4783 and an INF student only CSE-4777. Natural substitutions, all in semester 7: Data Engineering & Analytics or Web & Application Security for SWE students; Cloud Infrastructure & Virtualization for SWE or SEC students; IoT Systems & Edge AI for AID students.

Running the tracks. With five to eleven sections per intake and separate male and female sections, not every track will fill in every section group. A track runs when at least 20 students choose it; smaller groups are merged across sections, with theory delivered in a shared or blended session where needed. EMB is capped by lab equipment.

10. Industry Practice (CSE-4810, semester 8)

Related: Industry Practice, Semester-8 rule

Decisions: Decision 21

Vacations are too short for an internship, the intake is too large for every student to be placed on site, and the campus is far from employers. The course therefore requires at least 200 logged hours of supervised professional work in any of four modes:

ModeDescription
AOn-site or hybrid internship with a company
BRemote internship, or a verified part-time remote job
CVirtual studio: an on-campus team working a real backlog under an industry mentor
DOpen-source contribution sprint, assessed by accepted contributions

Semester 8 is built around it: only 11 scheduled hours per week, timetabled on two days, with the two theory courses suited to blended delivery. Students either do a six-week block at the start of the semester while classes run online, or work three days a week throughout. Semester-8 courses are planned accordingly: weeks 1–6 use only work that can be done remotely, and every supervised test, Level 0 quiz and viva falls in weeks 7–14 on campus. Assessment of Industry Practice is by logbook, supervisor's evaluation, a reflective report and a viva.

11. Capstone (CSE-4700 and CSE-4800)

Related: Capstone

Decisions: Decision 22

Teams of three or four work for two semesters on a problem in their track with a real client, user group or dataset. Capstone I (3 credits) ends with an approved proposal, a design and a working prototype. Capstone II (5 credits) ends with a deployed or demonstrable system, a report and an individual defence. A research thesis is allowed in place of a product for students heading to graduate study.

16. Resource implications

Related: Resources and planning

Decisions: Decision 29

  • Faculty. About 46 full-time CSE teachers, most of them lecturers. New or heavily revised courses (cloud, AI application engineering, security operations, infrastructure, verification) need training time or industry adjuncts before first delivery.
  • Labs. The existing computer labs cover every software course. The microprocessor labs need modern microcontroller kits, logic analysers and sensors. The EMB track additionally needs FPGA boards. The SEC track needs one department server, or a few repurposed PCs, for a shared log-analysis platform and an evidence store. The INF track needs a few managed switches and routers (or a network emulator), two or three hypervisor hosts, a teaching rack built from retired servers, and a small budget for cloud sandbox accounts so that students without a payment card can take part.
  • Purchases. The department has confirmed that the equipment above can be bought.
  • Fewer lab sections. Total lab and studio contact falls, which eases room and teacher load.
  • Industry Practice. A coordinator and a partner list are needed a year before the first cohort reaches semester 8.

18. Known sequencing issues

Related: Prerequisite graph

These were found while the outlines were written and are handled in the instructor notes of the courses concerned.

  • Physics and Electrical Circuits share semester 2. The circuits course uses the diode and the inductor before the physics course explains the p–n junction and induction. The circuits course therefore teaches device behaviour from measured characteristics, and physics supplies the explanation later in the same semester.
  • Complex numbers and densities before Calculus II. EEE-1225 (semester 2) uses phasors before MATH-2409 (semester 4) teaches complex numbers, so the circuits course keeps a short primer. Statistics (semester 3) keeps joint distributions discrete; MATH-2409 takes up continuous joint densities.
  • Prerequisite chain into semester 5. Operating Systems requires Computer Organization & Architecture, so a failure in semester 4 delays Operating Systems by a semester.
