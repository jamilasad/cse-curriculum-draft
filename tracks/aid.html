Every student in the programme already learns to build an AI feature with LLM APIs in the core studio (CSE-3660). This track is for depth: understanding and training models, engineering the data they depend on, and running and evaluating AI systems in production.

Jobs the track leads to

Entry-level machine-learning engineer posts tend to be fewer than the other three. Most graduates of the track should expect to start as AI application engineers, data engineers or analysts and move into model work with experience or graduate study. The track should be described to students in those terms.

Who it suits

The track needs stronger mathematics than the other four, and students should be told so before they choose. CSE-3673 uses matrix notation, gradients and probability from its second week. CSE-4773 derives backpropagation and attention and expects students to compute both by hand. A student who passed MATH-1209 and STAT-2313 by memorising procedures will struggle in both courses. AI assistants do not remove the difficulty, because the tests and vivas are at Level 0.

  • Well suited: students who did well in Linear Algebra & Numerical Computing, Probability & Statistics and the core AI course, who program comfortably in Python, and who are willing to read documentation and papers in English.
  • Possible with effort: mid-level students whose strength is SQL, careful data work and clear writing. They can do well in CSE-4783 and CSE-4883 and reach data-engineering and analyst jobs, but they must still pass CSE-3673 and CSE-4773.
  • Does not need this track: a student who mainly wants to build applications that call LLM APIs. The core studio and the SWE track already lead there with less mathematics.

A guide for advisers, for the department to confirm (the framework sets no grade rule): look for B or better in MATH-1209, STAT-2313 and CSE-3545. CSE-3673 holds an unmarked Level 0 diagnostic on linear algebra, probability and Python in week 1, early enough for a student to change track if the add/drop period allows.

How the four courses build on each other

  1. CSE-3673 Applied Machine Learning (S6) teaches the supervised-learning workflow on messy tabular data and sets the habits every later course relies on: a locked test set, a baseline before a model, validation that matches how the model will be used. It runs beside the studio on purpose. In the studio students build on models they did not train; here they train and validate their own.
  2. CSE-4773 Deep Learning & Generative AI (S7) opens up the models used in the studio: how networks are trained, how the transformer works, how a pre-trained model is adapted cheaply, and why language models fail. It reuses the validation discipline and the simple baselines of CSE-3673.
  3. CSE-4783 Data Engineering & Analytics (S7) covers the data side: modelling for analytics, pipelines, data quality, dashboards and governance. It does not depend on Track Courses I and II, so SWE students can take it as a substitution. For AID students it supplies the pipelines that the last course operates.
  4. CSE-4883 ML Systems: MLOps, Evaluation & Responsible AI (S8) takes a model from courses I and II and a pipeline from course III to production standard: versioned, served, monitored, evaluated, secured, checked for fairness and documented.

Four threads run through all of them: honest evaluation; "by hand once, then with tools"; work with Bangla-language data; and written documentation in English that grows from a model report in course I to a model card and risk assessment in course IV.

Compute, tools and AI use

  • Compute. The department has ordinary PC labs and no GPU cluster. The labs are planned around free notebook services, small models, parameter-efficient methods and hosted APIs under a spending cap. Each lab plan states what it runs on. Nothing in the track needs a model trained from scratch at scale.
  • Tools. Content is vendor-neutral. Model names, library versions and free quotas change every year, so the outlines name families and practices and give current tools only as examples. For each offering the instructor publishes a one-page list of the tools and models used that semester.
  • AI use. Lab work in CSE-3673, CSE-4773 and CSE-4883 is at Level 3: an AI system is the thing being built and measured. CSE-4783 works on data rather than on AI systems, so its lab work is at Level 2, except week 11, where AI-written SQL is the thing measured. Take-home assignments are at Level 2. AI coding assistants may be used in lab work under the Level 2 conditions (usage logged, output tested, the student defends every line). Exercises marked "by hand" are Level 1. All quizzes, written examinations, supervised practical tests and vivas are Level 0.

Suggested capstone direction

An AI or data product for a real organisation or user group, built on data the team collects or is given, with at least part of it in Bangla or from a Bangladeshi setting. A capstone in this track should show four things: a data pipeline the team built; a model the team trained, adapted or chose with evidence; an evaluation the team designed; and a deployed system with monitoring. Capstone Project I (S7) runs beside CSE-4773 and CSE-4783 and should end with the pipeline, a baseline and an evaluation set. Capstone Project II (S8) runs beside CSE-4883, whose lab project may be the operational layer of the capstone system.

Examples: a Bangla customer-support assistant for a local business, with retrieval, an evaluation suite and a cost sheet; demand forecasting with a dashboard for a pharmacy or retail chain; extraction of fields from Bangla forms and invoices; a churn or credit-risk model with a fairness audit; an analytics platform on public data. A thesis in place of a product could be a Bangla evaluation set with baselines, or a study of parameter-efficient fine-tuning for a low-resource task.

Substitution

CSE-3673 is Track Course I, the foundation of the track, and cannot be replaced (framework section 9). One of the semester-7 or semester-8 courses may be swapped, with the adviser's approval, for a course of another track offered in the same semester.

The framework names IoT Systems & Edge AI (CSE-4789 with CSE-4790, EMB track, S7) as the natural substitution for AID students. It suits a student whose capstone runs models on devices: crop or product inspection on a phone or microcontroller, sensor analytics. It should replace CSE-4783, not CSE-4773, because CSE-4883 cannot be followed without the deep-learning course. The cost is real: the student gives up data engineering, which is the most direct route to data-engineer and analyst jobs, and must complete the pipeline refresher in week 1 of the CSE-4884 lab. The adviser should also check the EMB course's own prerequisites.

In the other direction, CSE-4783 is the framework's named substitution for SWE students, and its outline is written so that it needs no machine-learning course beyond the core.

Substitution checker

Overlap. Track Course I (semester 6) is each track's foundation and cannot be replaced. A student may replace one of Track Courses II–IV with a course from another track offered in the same semester, with the adviser's approval. A course that is a prerequisite of the student's own semester-8 track course cannot be swapped; in practice an AID student may swap only CSE-4783 and an INF student only CSE-4777. Natural substitutions, all in semester 7: Data Engineering & Analytics or Web & Application Security for SWE students; Cloud Infrastructure & Virtualization for SWE or SEC students; IoT Systems & Edge AI for AID students.
