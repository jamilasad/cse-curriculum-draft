Rationale and what changed

The 2022 syllabus taught Financial and Managerial Accounting, Principles of Economics and Industrial Management as three general courses (6 credits) with no link to software work. This course keeps what a software engineer uses in the first years of work: planning and estimating a project, running it with a team, deciding with money in view, and understanding how a software business earns. It runs beside Capstone Project I, so each technique is applied in the same week to the student's own project. Entrepreneurship here includes the service firm and the freelancer as well as the product start-up, because service work for foreign clients is the main market for this programme's graduates. Left out: double-entry bookkeeping, macroeconomics, factory and production management, and the PMBOK process catalogue as a list to memorise.

Minimum competency

  • Given eight to ten activities with durations and dependencies, draw the network and find the critical path and the project duration by hand.
  • Produce a three-point estimate for a small backlog and a risk register of at least five risks, each with probability, impact, response and owner.
  • Compute the net present value and payback period of a cash flow of up to five years with a calculator and a supplied formula sheet, and say which option to choose.
  • From a supplied price sheet, compute the monthly bill of a small cloud deployment with an LLM feature and its cost per active user.
  • Write a fixed-price quotation for a small job: hours, rate, contingency, assumptions and exclusions.

Stretch goals

  • Simulate a schedule with uncertain task durations (Monte Carlo in a spreadsheet or Python) and report the date that has an 80% chance of being met.
  • Run customer discovery with ten or more real interviews and a landing-page test, and change the idea on the evidence.
  • Enter the venture in a business-plan or start-up competition, or win a first paying client for it.

Assessment

Written mid-term and final (80). The mid-term covers weeks 1–7 and the final the whole course with weight on weeks 8–14. A formula sheet, interest tables and all prices are supplied in the paper. At least half the marks are worked problems and cases of these kinds:

  • A table of ten activities: draw the network, find the critical path and float, and say what happens if one named activity slips three days.
  • A burndown chart and three sprints of velocity: will the release date hold, and what should the client be told?
  • A short case (a remote team missing deadlines; a client adding scope): identify the risks, choose responses and write the change-control decision.
  • Two options with four years of cash flows (build in-house against subscribe): compute net present value and payback at a given rate and recommend one.
  • A price sheet and a workload: compute the monthly cloud and AI bill and the cost per active user, and say which single change reduces it most.
  • A client brief and an estimate: write the fixed-price quotation, or argue for time-and-materials for this client.
  • A flawed lean canvas: name the untested assumptions and the cheapest test of each. A short income statement and cash position: can this firm pay salaries next month?

Recall (definitions of terms) is at most a fifth of the marks.

Continuous assessment (20). Quizzes at Level 0: four short in-class quizzes, best three counted (10). Assignments (10):

  • Assignment 1 (individual, 5): a spreadsheet cost and price model for the student's own capstone product (for a student not yet admitted to Capstone Project I, the CSE-3660 studio product): development cost at a loaded hourly rate, monthly cloud and AI cost at three usage levels from published prices, break-even price, and a build-versus-buy comparison for one component.
  • Assignment 2 (team of three or four, 5): venture mini-project: lean canvas backed by notes from at least five customer interviews, unit economics, and the week 14 pitch.

The capstone team's plan (breakdown, estimate, schedule, risk register) is drafted in class in weeks 2–5 and marked in CSE-4700, not here, so no work is marked twice.

Notes for the instructor

In the planned public view this section is shown to the committee only (specification 3.4).

  • The usual failure is a course of definitions. End every week with an artefact the student made: a network diagram, a register, a spreadsheet. Keep definition slides under a fifth of class time.
  • Weaker students fear the finance weeks. Use one running example from week 8 to week 13, give the formula sheet on the first day, and work every method by hand with a calculator before opening a spreadsheet.
  • Cloud and AI prices change every few months. Teach how to read a price sheet and build the model; never examine remembered prices.
  • AI tools produce a lean canvas, a risk register or a business plan in seconds. Mark the evidence behind them (interview notes, real quotations, real price sheets) and question each team on its assumptions at the pitch.
  • The campus is far from employers, so bring two guests online: a delivery manager from a service firm and a full-time freelancer or founder, each on how they estimate, price and get paid.

About this group of courses: Professional Practice, Capstone and Industry Practice

These six courses, all in semesters 7 and 8, carry most of the programme's evidence for outcomes PO6 to PO12; the technical courses carry PO1 to PO5. Each PO claimed here is tied to a named piece of marked work that can be kept on file for an accreditation visit. The courses feed one another: CSE-4753 and CSE-4760 run beside Capstone Project I and supply its plan, cost model, proposal and presentations, and CSE-4805 runs beside Capstone Project II and Industry Practice and takes its cases from them. Everything is specified for an intake of five to eleven sections twice a year (taken here as 200 to 300 students), so the supervision, panel and placement arithmetic is shown.
