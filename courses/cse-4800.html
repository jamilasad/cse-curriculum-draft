Rationale and what changed

Capstone II turns the approved design and prototype into a system that the client can use or that can be demonstrated on real data, evaluates it, and reports it. It follows the shared design set out under CSE-4700: the same team, client, cluster and supervisor, the same rules for the complex engineering problem, individual marks, AI use and the thesis alternative. The schedule allows for Industry Practice in the same semester and follows the semester-8 rule: weeks 1–6 hold only work that can be done remotely and the lighter milestones (block students join the cluster sessions and the alpha online), and the evaluation, the report and the final defence fall in weeks 7–14, on campus, when students who took the six-week block are back.

Minimum competency

  • Demonstrate a feature they built, running in the deployed system, and show the test that covers it.
  • Explain how the system was evaluated and what one result means for the client.
  • Name one risk the system poses to its users or to others and what the team did about it.
  • Explain any AI-assisted code in their own commits, as recorded in the usage log.

Stretch goals

  • The client is using the system at the end of the semester and has signed the acceptance form without conditions.
  • The evaluation compares against a baseline or uses a user study with tasks and enough participants to support its claim.
  • A paper, an open-source release or an entry in a national project competition comes out of the work.

Final defence. Forty-five minutes a team: fifteen for presentation and live demonstration, then about eight minutes of questions to each member, who opens the repository and explains their own commits, one part of the system they did not write, and one evaluation result. Each report is marked by the supervisor and one second marker.

Final report. One report per team, 25–40 pages on the department template: problem and client; requirements and complexity declaration revisited; design and alternatives; implementation; evaluation; impact, ethics and sustainability; project management (plan against actual, cost); value and continuation (who will run the system after hand-over, what it costs to keep, how it could be extended or offered to other clients); what each member learned and how; AI-use statement; a signed table of who did what.

Resources

As for CSE-4700, with a deployment target chosen for low cost: free tiers of cloud providers, the department's own server, or the client's hosting; the cloud and AI budget approved in the plan.

Notes for the supervisor

In the planned public view this section is shown to the committee only (specification 3.4).

  • Teams leave the report to week 13. Require the evaluation protocol in week 5 and the draft in week 11, and do not accept a beta without a deployed build.
  • Semester 8 also holds Industry Practice. Expect uneven effort in weeks 1–6 from members on a block, agree it in the week 1 plan, and judge contribution over the whole semester.
  • A system that works but was never evaluated does not show PO4. "Evaluation" for a mid-level team can be modest: ten users doing five tasks, with times and errors recorded, is enough if it is done honestly.
  • At the defence, ask each student about a commit chosen by the panel, not one chosen by the student.

About this group of courses: Professional Practice, Capstone and Industry Practice

These six courses, all in semesters 7 and 8, carry most of the programme's evidence for outcomes PO6 to PO12; the technical courses carry PO1 to PO5. Each PO claimed here is tied to a named piece of marked work that can be kept on file for an accreditation visit. The courses feed one another: CSE-4753 and CSE-4760 run beside Capstone Project I and supply its plan, cost model, proposal and presentations, and CSE-4805 runs beside Capstone Project II and Industry Practice and takes its cases from them. Everything is specified for an intake of five to eleven sections twice a year (taken here as 200 to 300 students), so the supervision, panel and placement arithmetic is shown.
