Rationale and what changed

A large part of security employment in banks, MFS providers and telecom operators is in risk, compliance and audit, and software exporters win foreign contracts partly by passing client security reviews. This course teaches that work as practice: assess risk, write controls that can be tested, audit them, plan for continuity, and apply the same discipline to cloud environments, where wrong identity settings and misconfiguration are the usual causes of failure. The 2022 syllabus had nothing comparable. Students score risks, write policy and read identity policies by hand before they use scanners, GRC tools or AI drafts. Law and professional ethics are taught in CSE-4805 in the same semester and are not repeated; the legal context of compliance is described in general terms only. Left out: clause-by-clause study of any standard, cloud architecture (covered in CSE-3653), and vendor-specific cloud security certificates.

Minimum competency

Every passing student can, unaided:

  1. Produce a risk register of at least eight risks for a given case, each with asset, threat, vulnerability, likelihood, impact, owner and treatment, and explain the scoring.
  2. Given a policy statement, write two testable controls and name the evidence an auditor would ask for.
  3. Read a cloud identity policy in JSON, say who can do what, and rewrite it to least privilege.
  4. Run a configuration scanner on a supplied infrastructure-as-code project, fix three findings and explain each.
  5. State a recovery time objective and a recovery point objective for a given service and choose a backup approach that meets them.

Stretch goals

  • Estimate two risks in money terms as ranges, in the manner of Hubbard and Seiersen, and argue for a control budget from the result.
  • Write policy-as-code checks (Open Policy Agent or a custom Checkov policy) that enforce one organisational rule in the pipeline.
  • Draft a statement of applicability against ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A for a small software firm.

Weekly plan — theory

Weeks 1–6 are delivered in blended form (a live online session plus recorded material) so that students on a six-week placement block can follow; weeks 7–14 are on campus.

Weekly plan — lab

CSE-4886 Security Governance, Risk & Cloud Security Lab · Lab · 1 credits · 2 contact hours a week

How the lab works with limited campus time:

  • The lab keeps its two scheduled hours on one of the two campus days. Labs marked (Remote) are document-based, need only a laptop and a spreadsheet, and run in the scheduled slot as a live online clinic for students on a placement block and in the lab room for the rest. Deliverables go to the course repository.
  • Labs marked (Campus) need the lab machines. Both supervised practical tests and the viva are in this part, after any six-week block has ended.
  • Cloud labs do not depend on paid accounts: configuration scanning works offline on supplied infrastructure-as-code, and live exercises reuse the cloud access arranged for CSE-3654. The lab room stays open on both campus days for students without a capable laptop.

Assessment

Theory. Mid-term and final (80). At least half the marks are application and analysis; recall is at most a fifth. Typical questions:

  • A one-page case: identify five risks, score them, choose a treatment for each and justify it.
  • A vague policy paragraph: rewrite it as controls that an auditor could test, and name the evidence.
  • A table of audit evidence: decide which controls fail and write one finding in full.
  • Loss figures and a control's cost: compute annualised loss before and after and decide whether the control is worth buying.
  • An identity policy in JSON: say what it allows, find the excess permission and correct it.
  • A service with stated business needs: set recovery objectives and choose a backup and recovery approach.

Continuous assessment (20): Level 0 quizzes held on campus from week 7, best three of four counted (10); two assignments (10), a completed client security questionnaire and a one-page risk memo to management.

Lab (100). Weekly lab work and lab record 30; two supervised practical tests at Level 0, 30; lab project 25; individual viva 15. Project (teams of three): a security assessment and improvement plan for a second organisation. This is either Case B, a fictional 60-person software exporter preparing for a foreign client's security review, with its cloud environment supplied as code, or the student's Industry Practice host with written consent and names removed. Deliverables are a scoped risk assessment, a gap assessment against the first implementation group of the CIS Controls, a cloud configuration review with fixes, and a prioritised 90-day plan presented to a panel acting as management. A student who cannot explain the scores and recommendations in the viva does not pass the lab.

Resources

  • Textbooks: Michael E. Whitman and Herbert J. Mattord, Management of Information Security; Evan Wheeler, Security Risk Management; Chris Dotson, Practical Cloud Security; Douglas W. Hubbard and Richard Seiersen, How to Measure Anything in Cybersecurity Risk; Chris Davis and Mike Schiller, IT Auditing: Using Controls to Protect Information Assets.
  • Standards and free references: NIST Cybersecurity Framework, NIST SP 800-30 and SP 800-53, CIS Critical Security Controls and CIS Benchmarks, CSA Cloud Controls Matrix, PCI DSS. ISO/IEC 27001, 27002, 27005 and ISO 22301 are paid documents; one library copy of each is enough.
  • Software (all free): a spreadsheet for the risk register, with eramba or CISO Assistant as an optional GRC tool; Terraform or OpenTofu, Checkov, Trivy, Prowler, Open Policy Agent, Keycloak, Wazuh, Gophish, restic, Docker.

Notes for the instructor

In the planned public view this section is shown to the committee only (specification 3.4).

  • The course fails if it becomes a list of frameworks to memorise. Tie every week to something the student produces: a register, a policy, a finding, a fixed configuration.
  • Mid-level students often do well here, because the work rewards care and clear writing. Give templates and one worked example of each document, a glossary in plain English, and allow a first draft in Bangla before the English version.
  • AI tools produce a generic risk register or policy in seconds, and practitioners use them for first drafts. Assessment therefore marks fit to the case: every risk must cite a fact from the case, every policy clause must be testable, and the viva asks why a score was chosen. Placement-host data and client documents are never put into an external AI service; this rule is itself an example of the acceptable-use policy taught in week 14.
  • Publish all materials in week 1 and agree the two campus days with the Industry Practice coordinator before the semester starts. Check the licence terms and free-tier limits of the cloud access each year; identity exercises need an environment that enforces policies, which some local emulators do not.
