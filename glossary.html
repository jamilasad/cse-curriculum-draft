Glossary

Terms used in the curriculum files and on this site. Each definition says where the term is set out.

AI-use level

How much help from AI tools a student may use in one assessed piece of work. Level 0: no AI help. Level 1: AI as a tutor that explains but does not write the solution. Level 2: AI as a pair programmer, with the use logged and every line defended. Level 3: AI is itself the subject of the work. Set in framework section 6.

BAETE

Board of Accreditation for Engineering and Technical Education, the body that accredits engineering programmes in Bangladesh. Its criteria are checked in framework section 15.

Bloom level

How demanding a learning outcome is, on a six-step scale: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyse, Evaluate, Create. Every CLO names one level.

Capstone

The final-year team project, over two semesters: Capstone Project I (CSE-4700, 3 credits) and Capstone Project II (CSE-4800, 5 credits). Teams of three or four work for a real client, user group or dataset in their track. Framework section 11.

CLO

Course learning outcome: one thing a student can do after passing a course. Each course has four to six CLOs, and each CLO maps to one or two POs.

Co-requisite

A course that must be taken in the same semester as another, or earlier. The outlines say "taken alongside", "taken with" or "taken in the same semester".

Contact hour

One scheduled hour a week in a classroom or lab. A theory credit is one contact hour; a lab credit is two (framework section 4).

Core Competency Assessment

CSE-2440, a one-credit, pass/fail practical examination in semester 4, taken on lab machines with no AI help. A pass is needed before the semester-7 track courses and Capstone I. Framework section 8.

Credit

The unit for counting a course towards the degree. The degree needs 142 credits in this draft, 13 of them general education.

EA

Engineering activities (EA1–EA5): the accreditation body's description of complex engineering activities. Draft 1 does not yet map them to courses.

GED

General education: university courses with GE or UR codes, such as Advanced English. They carry credit and are kept as in 2022.

Gate

A course that must be passed before others can be taken. In this draft the gate is the Core Competency Assessment (CSE-2440).

Industry Practice

CSE-4810 in semester 8: at least 200 logged hours of supervised professional work, in one of four modes (on-site internship, remote work, virtual studio, open-source sprint). Framework section 10.

Lab-only course

A course with no theory part, credited like a lab: Developer Tools & Technical Communication Lab (CSE-1130) and Technical & Professional Communication (CSE-4760).

MDP

Non-credit university courses with MDP codes. They take contact hours but carry no credit.

Minimum competency

The skills every passing student must show unaided. Each outline lists three to five.

Paired lab

The lab part of a theory course. Theory and lab are parts of one course (decision 11) and share one page on this site.

Prerequisite

A course that must be passed before another can be taken. On this site, prerequisites are read from the "Prerequisites" row of each outline.

PO

Programme outcome: one of twelve abilities every graduate must have (PO1–PO12), set by the accreditation body. Listed on the outcomes page.

Semester-8 rule

Semester 8 has only 11 scheduled hours a week, on two days, so that students can do Industry Practice. Weeks 1–6 use work that can be done remotely; every supervised test, Level 0 quiz and viva is held on campus in weeks 7–14. Framework section 10.

Studio

A project course with no theory part, where credit comes from scheduled studio hours plus logged project hours, 25 hours per credit. The two studios are CSE-2434 and CSE-3660. Framework section 4.

Track

A group of four courses in one job family, taken in semesters 6 to 8. There are five: SWE, AID, SEC, INF and EMB. Framework section 9.

Track Course I–IV

The four slots of a track. Course I is in semester 6 and cannot be replaced; II and III are in semester 7; IV is in semester 8. One of II–IV may be swapped for a course of another track in the same semester.

Viva

A short oral examination in which a student explains their own work. Every lab and studio ends with one; a student who cannot explain their own work does not pass the lab.

WK

Knowledge profile (WK1–WK9): the accreditation body's list of the kinds of knowledge a programme must teach. Draft 1 does not yet map them to courses.

WP

Complex problem attributes (WP1–WP7): what makes an engineering problem "complex" for the accreditation body. Draft 1 does not yet map them to courses.
